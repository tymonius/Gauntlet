Design basis

What this pass changes

Adaptive composition

The framed illustration uses the largest approved size that the rules permit. Dense cards reduce art before reducing otherwise readable text.

Rules hierarchy

Action and Battle labels occupy a fixed left column while Adobe Caslon rules align in a wider right column. Territory effects use the same reading face.

Shared identity

Playable cards and Territories share the ivory shell, layered keylines, parchment, title face, artwork frame, and restrained three-part footer.

Overlay identification

Territory Overlays preserve the normal title and repeat it vertically in a widened faction-colored rail for sideways table use.

Print and compare

Four playable-card conditions

The same portrait system adapts its illustration height to each card. The first three specimens use finished artwork to test sparse, dense, and reminder layouts; Encampment adds the Territory Overlay rail and its three-section rules structure while its illustration remains pending.

Physical review

What to inspect after printing

  1. Whether each illustration reaches a satisfying size without forcing the rules below a comfortable reading threshold.
  2. Whether the finished paintings integrate naturally with the ivory border, parchment field, and restrained frame.
  3. Whether all-caps P22 1722 gives the cards a strong common silhouette without making long titles feel compressed.
  4. Whether the Action and Battle columns align cleanly and remain easier to scan than a single full-width rules column.
  5. Whether the value medallion feels anchored by its shared alignment with the illustration frame.
  6. Whether Encampment remains legible both upright and sideways, and whether the widened Military rail is prominent without overwhelming the ordinary card face.
  7. Whether High Ground reads as an unmistakable landscape sibling of the playable cards, with enough illustration presence to avoid becoming a text-only rules tile.
