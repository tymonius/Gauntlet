# Gauntlet v0.6.1 Faction Introductions

> **First-game onboarding aid.** Read or show these introductions before new players choose factions. They explain how each faction generally feels without replacing the rulebook, faction guides, Leader cards, or reference materials.

# Choosing a faction

Every faction can win by running the Gauntlet: advancing across the battlefield, capturing the opponent's final Territory, and winning the Last Stand battle beyond it.

Five factions also have an additional victory condition. Those plans do not remove the need to move, fight, occupy Territories, and respond to the opponent. They give each faction a different way to create pressure while pursuing the same territorial contest.

For a first game, choose the faction whose decisions sound most appealing rather than the one that sounds strongest.

---

# Military

## How it feels

Military is the most direct faction. Win battles to gain **Command**, then spend Command on **Orders** that strengthen attacks or defenses, force deeper retreats, capture ground faster, or continue an offensive into another battle.

Military has no separate victory condition. Everything it does supports the central objective of running the Gauntlet.

## Choose Military if…

- you want clear battlefield goals;
- you enjoy attacking, defending, and controlling the pace of combat;
- you want faction abilities that are easy to connect to movement and battle; or
- you prefer winning through territorial pressure rather than building a separate engine.

## First-game focus

Do not spend Command simply because it is available. Decide whether the current battle is worth strengthening or whether saving Command could create a stronger capture or follow-up attack later.

### General

Choose the **General** for forward pressure. The General can move farther before battle, strengthen battles they initiate, and continue advancing after a victory.

### Commandant

Choose the **Commandant** for defense and counterattack. The Commandant strengthens battles initiated by the opponent, forces deeper retreats, and can turn a defensive victory into immediate capture.

# Diplomats

## How it feels

Diplomats may offer **Terms** before a battle begins. The opponent can accept the Proposal and avoid the battle, or refuse it and fight with Influence at stake. Accepted or successfully imposed Proposals become **Treaty Articles**.

Complete five different Treaty Articles to win through the **Peace Treaty**, or use negotiation and Influence to create a path toward running the Gauntlet.

## Choose Diplomats if…

- you enjoy negotiation and reading the opponent;
- you want meaningful decisions before battles begin;
- you like offering choices in which either answer can benefit you; or
- you want an additional victory condition that the opponent can see developing.

## First-game focus

A Proposal is not automatically good because it is accepted. Offer Terms when acceptance helps your position and refusal creates a battle you are prepared to fight.

### Ambassador

Choose the **Ambassador** for agreement and card flow. Accepted Terms help the Ambassador maintain momentum and keep making offers.

### Senator

Choose the **Senator** for higher-stakes political pressure. The Senator can protect staked Influence after losing a battle by sacrificing cards from Hand.

# Financiers

## How it feels

Financiers build **Capital**, place cards in a **Treasury**, and buy **Deeds** connected to Territories. Treasury cards increase the amount of Capital you can keep, while owned Deeds generate income.

Deed ownership is separate from Territory control. Own every Deed currently in the Gauntlet to win through **Controlling Interest**.

## Choose Financiers if…

- you enjoy building an economic engine;
- you like converting cards, Territory control, and timing into long-term value;
- you want to invest now for stronger options later; or
- you enjoy pursuing several connected resources at once.

## First-game focus

Capital that cannot be kept at the end of the turn is temporary. Watch your Capital limit, and decide whether a valuable card is more useful in Hand, in Treasury, or as collateral.

### Banker

Choose the **Banker** for planned purchases and flexible financing. The Banker may use a card from Hand or Treasury as collateral for the first Deed purchase each turn.

### Executive

Choose the **Executive** for offensive acquisition. After winning as the attacker and entering Occupation on an enemy Territory, the Executive can buy its Deed and convert the purchase into immediate control.

# Intelligence

## How it feels

Intelligence begins and completes hidden **Missions** to earn **Intel** and **Operation Progress**. During battle, Intel powers **Surveillance**, which reveals opposing Gambits or Tactics, and **Interference**, which removes a revealed choice and forces a replacement or pass.

Build enough Operation Progress to begin a hidden **Special Operation**. Satisfy its Mission requirement and pay the Intel cost to win.

## Choose Intelligence if…

- you enjoy hidden objectives and information advantages;
- you want to inspect and disrupt the opponent's battle choices;
- you like adapting after learning what the opponent planned; or
- you enjoy managing several small objectives toward a larger operation.

## First-game focus

Intel is valuable because it can affect both the Gambit stage and the Tactic stage. Do not spend it merely to learn information that will not change your decision.

### Ranger

Choose the **Ranger** for field operations and Territory control. The Ranger can spend Intel to ignore a printed Territory effect for the rest of the turn.

### Spymaster

Choose the **Spymaster** for Mission tempo. After completing a normal Mission, the Spymaster can immediately begin another without using an Action Opportunity.

# Mystics

## How it feels

Mystics complete three public **Rites** by sacrificing, binding, recovering, and exchanging cards. The first completed Rite unlocks **Invocation**, the second unlocks **Transmutation**, and the third unlocks **Convergence** and the final **Ritual of Ascendance**.

After completing all three Rites, bind one Arcane card from Hand, one from the Discard Pile, and one from the Graveyard. Initiate and win a battle while those cards remain bound to win through the Ritual.

## Choose Mystics if…

- you enjoy planning across several turns;
- you like using the Graveyard as an active resource;
- you want sacrifices and card destinations to create new opportunities; or
- you enjoy a visible, interruptible progression toward a dramatic final battle.

## First-game focus

Do not treat every card entering the Graveyard as a loss. Mystics can recover value from that zone, but committing too many useful cards too early can still leave you without the right tools for a Rite or battle.

### Alchemist

Choose the **Alchemist** for card conversion. The first qualifying sacrifice from Hand during the turn draws a replacement card.

### Spirit Walker

Choose the **Spirit Walker** for Rite endurance. The Spirit Walker may sacrifice an Arcane card to protect a begun Rite from the first qualifying battle-loss interruption during the turn.

# Inquisition

## How it feels

The Inquisition gains **Conviction** when opposing cards enter the Graveyard during the Aftermath of a battle. **Condemnation** sends opposing Tactics to the Graveyard instead of the Discard Pile, while **Blasphemy** grants additional Conviction when the opponent uses Arcane cards.

Spend Conviction on **Purges** that remove cards and Assets. If the opponent cannot make their normal start-of-turn draw because both their Draw Pile and Discard Pile are empty, win through **Purification**.

## Choose Inquisition if…

- you enjoy attrition and permanent resource pressure;
- you want the opponent's battle choices to become increasingly costly;
- you like spending a small, tightly limited resource on targeted removal; or
- you enjoy forcing the opponent to plan around dwindling recyclable cards.

## First-game focus

Conviction has a maximum of four. Spend it deliberately, but avoid sitting at the maximum while additional gains are about to occur.

### Grand Inquisitor

Choose the **Grand Inquisitor** for judgment and Purge efficiency. After winning a battle and moving cards to their destinations, the Grand Inquisitor may perform a discounted Purge.

### Witch Hunter

Choose the **Witch Hunter** for defense and pursuit. After defeating an attacking opponent, the Witch Hunter can spend Conviction to end their turn and immediately pursue them.

---

# First-game recommendation

For the simplest first exposure to faction systems, begin with **Military** against a faction whose additional victory condition interests the other player. Use the recommended Deck for each selected Leader and keep the faction reference material visible.

After the game, record whether each introduction accurately prepared the player for the faction's decisions and whether the recommended Deck behaved as described.
