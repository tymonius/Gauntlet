# Gauntlet v0.6.1 — First Playtest Revision

**Release date:** To be assigned at publication  
**Status:** Draft pre-release playtest edition

Gauntlet v0.6.1 is the first revision built from physical v0.6.0 playtest evidence. It preserves the six-faction framework and the existing card and Territory pools while replacing the battle vocabulary and timing structure, improving onboarding, and synchronizing the full card pool with a more exact shared rules system.

## Why this revision exists

The first physical v0.6.0 session was highly positive but exposed several avoidable barriers:

- players needed clearer names for the different cards involved in battle;
- reveal order and effect timing were difficult to infer consistently;
- withdrawal, retreat, cleanup, and post-battle movement were not separated precisely enough;
- Hand, battle-drawn cards, Graveyard, Discard Pile, Assets, and faction components needed clearer physical organization;
- the Rules Arbiter needed stricter citation and uncertainty standards; and
- the playtest workflow needed to separate teaching time, game time, external interruptions, and completed-game evidence.

The session ended because a player had to leave. Its roughly 90-minute duration, ten rounds, and three battles are retained as onboarding and process evidence, not as a completed-game pacing or balance result.

## New battle language

Battles now use three distinct card roles:

- **Gambit:** an optional card set from Hand before temporary cards are drawn. Gambits normally go to the Graveyard.
- **Reserve:** three temporary cards drawn for that battle.
- **Tactic:** an optional card chosen from Reserve. Tactics normally go to the Discard Pile.

Cards may now carry **Gambit**, **Tactic**, or **Battle** headings. A Battle effect is flexible and may be used in either role.

The core memory aid is:

> **Gambits go to the Graveyard.**

## New battle sequence

Each battle now follows an explicit eight-step structure:

1. opening effects;
2. set Gambits;
3. form Reserves;
4. reveal Gambits;
5. choose Tactics;
6. reveal Tactics;
7. resolve the battle; and
8. resolve the Aftermath of the battle.

The attacker normally acts first at both hidden-choice stages. Effects may change Gambit order or Tactic order independently.

## Clearer reference rules

v0.6.1 adds shared procedures for:

- simultaneous reveal and attacker-first alternating effect resolution;
- early reveal without early resolution;
- negation before, but not after, resolution;
- replacing Gambits and Tactics;
- revising choices without reopening information windows;
- multiple and additional Tactics;
- copied effects and source-dependent instructions;
- Asset replacement at capacity;
- Overlay control following Territory control;
- effect-caused withdrawal;
- forced retreat after a loss;
- the complete Aftermath order; and
- post-battle movement and follow-up battles.

## A rulebook that teaches before it qualifies

Every major section now begins with **How it works**, a clear-language explanation of the normal case. It is followed by **Complete rules**, the authoritative treatment of timing, edge cases, and unusual interactions.

This structure is intended to let a new player learn the game by reading the opening explanation of each section while still letting experienced players use the same document as a comprehensive reference.

## Faction updates

All six factions were audited against the new battle sequence.

### Military

Orders now use exact Movement and Aftermath timing. Military cards involving chain battles, additional battle cards, pursuit, capture, and retreat have been rewritten around Gambits, Reserves, Tactics, and the Aftermath.

### Diplomats

Terms now occur during opening effects before Gambits. Accepted Terms prevent the pending battle. A mirror procedure establishes attacker-first priority when both players are Diplomats.

### Financiers

Deed ownership, collateral, Treasury use, and Deed purchases are unchanged in principle but now use exact Action Opportunity and Aftermath timing.

### Intelligence

Surveillance may be used once during the Gambit stage and once during the Tactic stage. Interference costs 2 Intel per target, returns the card to its source, and permits replacement from that source without opening a new information opportunity.

### Mystics

Completing three Rites no longer wins immediately. The third Rite unlocks Convergence and permits the Mystic to begin the Ritual of Ascendance. The Mystic must bind three Arcane cards, initiate a battle, and win while they remain bound.

### Inquisition

Conviction, Condemnation, Final Judgment, Tyranny, Heresy, Hellfire, and related effects now use exact reveal and Aftermath timing. Confession has been redesigned for the Tactic stage.

## Card and Territory synchronization

The Neutral pool, all six faction pools, and all 25 Territories have been revised for the new terminology and timing model.

The revision does not add or remove card titles. The release still contains:

- 50 Neutral cards;
- 72 faction cards;
- 25 Territories, including four Arenas;
- six factions; and
- twelve Leaders.

## Playtest workflow

Formal printed playtest sheets are planned to carry unique single-use QR codes linked to digital sessions. Rules Arbiter questions asked during a session will be stored with the session, rules version, citations, and reviewer status. A short sheet serial will provide fallback reconciliation.

The playtest record now distinguishes:

- instruction and setup time;
- game time;
- total session time;
- completed games;
- concessions;
- external interruptions;
- rules blockers; and
- component or technical failures.

## Balance status

v0.6.1 is primarily a clarity, timing, onboarding, and consistency update. The first physical game did not provide enough completed-game evidence to justify faction or matchup balance changes.

Balance, experienced-game duration, comeback health, and victory-route pacing remain active test questions for completed v0.6.1 games.

## Canonical sources

At publication, the v0.6.1 rulebook, six faction guides, Neutral Card Pool, and Territory Pool will be the authoritative player-facing sources. Canonical JSON, references, printable sheets, browser tools, and digital implementations must be regenerated from those governing sources rather than edited independently.
