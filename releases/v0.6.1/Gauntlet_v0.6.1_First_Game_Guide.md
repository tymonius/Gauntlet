# Gauntlet v0.6.1 First-Game Guide

> **Teaching aid for a player's first game.** This guide explains the ordinary flow of play and a recommended teaching sequence. The Official Rulebook and exact card text remain authoritative.

# The goal

The battlefield is a line of six Territory cards called the **Gauntlet**. Each player starts immediately before their own end.

Advance across the Territories, defeat the opponent, capture their final Territory, then advance beyond the Gauntlet and win the **Last Stand** battle to win.

Five factions may also win through a faction-specific plan. Those plans create additional pressure, but movement, battle, occupation, and Territory control remain central to every game.

# Before choosing factions

1. Read or show the relevant entries in the [Faction Introductions](Gauntlet_v0.6.1_Faction_Introductions.md).
2. Let each player choose the faction whose decisions sound most appealing.
3. Choose one of that faction's two Leaders.
4. Use the recommended Deck for that Leader.
5. Keep the faction guide or reference card visible.

For the most direct first game, at least one player should use **Military**. Military has no additional victory condition and demonstrates movement, battle, Command, retreat, occupation, and capture clearly.

# Set up the table

Give each player a separate visible area for:

- Draw Pile;
- Discard Pile;
- Graveyard;
- Hand;
- Asset Bank;
- Leader and faction components;
- Reserve;
- Gambit; and
- Tactic.

The printable [Player Mat](../../playtest/player-mat/) labels these areas and shows the battle sequence.

Then:

1. Shuffle each 30-card Playable Deck.
2. Secretly arrange each player's three Territories.
3. Join them into one six-Territory line and reveal them.
4. Place each Player Token immediately before that player's end.
5. Prepare Leaders and faction components.
6. Draw three cards into each Hand.
7. Roll to determine the first player; reroll ties.

# Teach the zones first

Before the first turn, show the physical difference between these four card areas:

## Hand

Your Hand is persistent and private. It remains with you from turn to turn. You normally discard down to three cards at the end of your turn.

## Gambit

A Gambit is a card set from Hand at the beginning of a battle. It gives you certainty because you chose it before seeing the temporary battle cards, but it normally goes to the Graveyard afterward.

> **Gambits go to the Graveyard.**

## Reserve

After Gambits are set, place the Hand physically aside and draw three temporary cards to form the Reserve. The Reserve is hidden from the opponent but separate from the Hand.

## Tactic

After Gambits are revealed, choose a Tactic from the Reserve. A Tactic normally goes to the Discard Pile afterward. Cards left in Reserve also go to the Discard Pile.

# A normal turn

On your turn:

1. **Capture.** If you still occupy an opposing Territory from the previous turn, rotate it toward you and take control.
2. **Draw.** Draw one card.
3. **Action before movement.** You may use your normal Action Opportunity now.
4. **Movement.** Advance, hold, or withdraw one position.
5. **Action after movement.** Use the normal Action Opportunity here only if you did not use it before movement.
6. **Cleanup.** Apply end-of-turn effects and discard down to three cards in Hand.

A player has one normal Action Opportunity, not one before movement and another after movement.

# Movement and Territory progress

## Advance

Move one position toward the opponent's end.

## Hold

Remain where you are.

## Withdraw

Move one position toward your own end.

Entering the opponent's position starts a battle. The moving player is the attacker.

A battle ends the movement that started it. Winning does not automatically allow further movement. A player moves again only when an Order, card, or rule explicitly permits it.

When an attacker wins on a Territory:

- the defender retreats;
- the attacker occupies that Territory; and
- the defender gets their next turn to counterattack.

If the attacker still occupies the Territory at the start of their next turn, they capture it.

# A normal battle

Teach the first battle slowly and use the labeled battle areas.

1. **Opening effects.** Establish the attacker, defender, and contested position. apply effects such as Terms.
2. **Set Gambits.** Attacker sets up to one eligible Gambit or passes. Defender then does the same.
3. **Form Reserves.** Set Hands aside and draw three cards each.
4. **Reveal Gambits.** Turn all Gambits face up and apply their effects.
5. **Choose Tactics.** Attacker chooses up to one eligible Tactic or passes. Defender then does the same.
6. **Reveal Tactics.** Turn all Tactics face up and apply their effects.
7. **Determine the Outcome.** Apply before-dice effects, roll, and compare battle totals.
8. **Aftermath.** Carry out retreat and Occupation, apply other result effects, move cards to their destinations, and carry out follow-up movement.

## Advantage and disadvantage

Each net advantage adds one die; use the highest result. Each net disadvantage adds one die; use the lowest result. They cancel one-for-one.

## Ties

When the defender controls the contested Territory, the defender wins a tied battle total. The defender also has this advantage during a Last Stand. Otherwise, reroll until the tie is broken.

# Aftermath and destinations

After determining the winner:

- a losing attacker returns to the position from which they entered;
- a losing defender retreats one position toward their own end;
- a winning attacker occupies the contested position;
- a winning defender remains there;
- Gambits normally go to the Graveyard;
- Tactics normally go to the Discard Pile; and
- cards remaining in Reserve go to the Discard Pile.

Card text may change these destinations.

# Withdrawal is not retreat

A player **retreats** because they lost a battle.

An effect may instead cause a player to **withdraw**. Withdrawal ends the battle without a winner or loser. Win, loss, and retreat triggers do not occur.

This distinction matters for many faction abilities and card effects.

# Assets and Overlays

## Assets

An Asset is a persistent card banked in the Asset Bank. The Asset limit equals the number of Territories that player controls.

When banking a new Asset at the limit, a player may normally discard one controlled Asset to make room during the same Action Opportunity.

## Overlays

An Overlay is placed on a Territory. It is controlled by whoever controls the Territory beneath it, although its owner does not change.

When control of the Territory changes, control of the Overlay changes with it.

# Running the Gauntlet

To win normally:

1. win a battle on the opponent's final Territory;
2. remain there through the opponent's counterattack opportunity;
3. capture that final Territory at the start of a later turn;
4. later advance beyond the Gauntlet, starting a Last Stand battle; and
5. win that Last Stand as the attacker.

If the defender wins the Last Stand, the attacker retreats and the game continues.

# How to teach card text

Do not explain every card before play. Instead:

- explain the printed headings on cards in each player's opening Hand;
- explain a new faction component when it first becomes relevant;
- let the **How it works** portion of the rulebook answer ordinary questions;
- use **Complete rules** for timing, replacements, simultaneous effects, and edge cases; and
- use the Rules Arbiter only when the governing sources need to be searched during play.

When a card has several printed effects, using one effect does not activate the others.

# What to record after the game

Use the formal [Playtest Sheet](../../playtest/) and record:

- instruction and setup time;
- game time;
- total session time;
- rules lookup time;
- whether the game completed or stopped;
- the reason for a stopped session;
- whether the faction introduction prepared each player;
- rules or table-organization problems; and
- the strongest and weakest moments of the game.

A session stopped by an external interruption remains useful for onboarding and qualitative feedback, but should not be counted as a completed-game pacing or balance result.
