Military · faction guide

Win the fight. Keep moving.

Military turns battlefield victories into Command, then spends that Command on Orders that extend movement, strengthen attacks or defenses, drive enemies back, and secure captured ground.

At the table

How Military plays.

Military is the most direct expression of Gauntlet’s central conflict: fight for the line, turn victory into momentum, and keep pressure on the opponent before they can recover.

Battle-driven

Your first victory each turn generates Command—even when it happens while defending during the opponent’s turn.

Fast tactical economy

Command tops out at 2, so the faction rewards spending it at the right moment rather than hoarding it.

Leader-shaped tempo

The General converts Command into forward pressure. The Commandant converts it into defense, retaliation, and control.

A good fit for you if

Choose Military when...

  • You want battles to drive your entire game plan.
  • You enjoy choosing between immediate pressure and holding resources for a stronger turn.
  • You want your Leader choice to create a clear offensive or defensive identity.

Signature system

The mechanics that define the faction.

01

Command

The first time each turn you win a battle, gain 1 Command, up to 2.

02

Orders

Spend Command on the three Orders printed on your Leader Card. Their timing determines whether they extend movement, modify a battle, increase retreat, or advance your Front Line.

03

Pressure tools

Military cards reinforce victories, add cards after reveal, establish Encampments, punish retreat, and create carefully limited follow-up battles.

Victory

How Military wins.

Run the Gauntlet

Military has no additional victory condition. Run the Gauntlet immediately by capturing the Territory at the opponent’s end or by forcing the opponent to make a Last Stand and winning the resulting battle.

The advantage

Because every Military system supports movement, battle, retreat, or Front Line advancement, the faction never has to divide its attention between the board and a separate victory engine.

Faction Leaders

Two ways to lead.

Each Leader uses the same faction card pool and core system, but pushes it toward a different tactical identity.

Attack, forward pressure, and tempo

General

“Forward. Again.”

The General leads from the decisive point, using Command to advance, reinforce an attack, and press a defeated opponent before they can recover.

Leader ability

Orders: Onward adds one Position during Movement before a pending battle is created; Rally adds +1 before dice in a battle the General initiated; Rout advances one Position at the end of the Aftermath after an attacking victory and may create a new pending battle.

Defense, counterattack, and control

Commandant

“We hold. They break.”

The Commandant turns a defended position into lasting control, strengthening defenses, driving attackers farther back, and advancing the Front Line after a qualifying victory.

Leader ability

Orders: Entrench adds +1 before dice in a battle the Commandant did not initiate; Repel adds one Position to the defeated attacker’s retreat after their normal retreat; Fortify advances the Front Line by one Territory after a victory while occupying enemy-controlled ground, if able.

Archetype and setting

An institution—and a method of power.

Military represents organized force: field armies, provincial forces, militias, garrisons, fortifications, supply networks, and the officers who turn political authority into control of the battlefield. The General embodies personal command, maneuver, morale, and decisive campaigning; the Commandant embodies discipline, readiness, fortification, logistics, and the permanent military establishment.

Leader inspirations: General — George Washington and Napoleon Bonaparte, drawing on the tradition of the heroic mounted field commander. Commandant — Baron von Steuben, Frederick the Great, and the severe eighteenth-century garrison or fortress commander.

Gauntlet takes place in an eighteenth-century powder-and-parchment world of competing provinces, chartered territories, autonomous cities, commercial possessions, and independent native nations. The six factions are institutional spheres that cross political and cultural borders: methods of exercising power rather than six countries or peoples.
