Inquisition · faction guide

Turn every loss into judgment.

The Inquisition makes attrition permanent. Opposing Tactics can be condemned to the Graveyard, those losses build Conviction, and Conviction fuels Purges that dismantle the opponent's remaining resources.

At the table

How the Inquisition plays.

The Inquisition wins through attrition and denial. Even an ordinary battle can permanently thin the opponent's options, creating pressure that grows whether or not the battlefield moves quickly.

Make battle costly

Opposing Tactics can be condemned to the Graveyard instead of recycling through the Discard Pile, turning normal battle spending into permanent loss.

Turn loss into authority

Those permanent losses build Conviction, which powers the faction's harsher effects. Attrition itself becomes a source of momentum.

Dismantle recovery

Purges turn Conviction into permanent removal from Assets, Hand, and other recoverable resources, making each later turn harder for the opponent to rebuild.

A good fit for you if

Choose Inquisition when...

  • You enjoy attrition, denial, and making resources permanently disappear.
  • You want battles to damage the opponent's future options as well as their position.
  • You prefer a faction victory created by steadily exhausting the opponent's recoverable Deck.

Signature system

What makes the faction different.

01

Conviction

Conviction is the momentum of the Inquisition's attrition plan. Permanent opposing losses—and some especially provocative enemy actions—can build it.

02

Condemnation and Purge

Condemnation keeps battle cards from recycling; Purge spends Conviction to remove larger pieces of the opponent's engine. Together they make recovery progressively harder.

03

Purification

If the opponent finally exhausts both their Draw Pile and Discard Pile and cannot make the normal draw, the Inquisition wins.

Victory

How the Inquisition wins.

Run the Gauntlet

Permanent removal and retaliation can clear a direct path through the Territories while shrinking the opponent's ability to resist.

Purification

Leave the opponent unable to make their normal start-of-turn draw because both their Draw Pile and Discard Pile are empty.

Faction Leaders

Two ways to lead.

Each Leader uses the same Conviction and Purge tools, but one turns victories into harsher judgment while the other weaponizes the opponent's failed attacks.

Judgment, Purge, and permanent removal

Grand Inquisitor

“We judge. We purge.”

The Grand Inquisitor turns battle victories into cheaper Purges, linking territorial success directly to permanent resource damage.

Why it matters

The Grand Inquisitor rewards choosing battles you can win, then using the victory to remove the opponent's most important recoverable resource.

Defense, retaliation, and pursuit

Witch Hunter

“You ran. I followed.”

The Witch Hunter can turn a failed enemy attack into an immediate pursuit, converting defense into counteroffense.

Why it matters

The Witch Hunter rewards holding enough Conviction in reserve to threaten pursuit. Opponents have to think twice before attacking a position he can defend.

Archetype and setting

An institution—and a method of power.

The Inquisition represents a rigorist religious-legal institution concerned with doctrine, spiritual danger, investigation, judgment, and suppression. Its authority is recognized differently from one jurisdiction to another, and its claims can conflict sharply with local civil authority and native sovereignty. The Grand Inquisitor embodies tribunal and institutional judgment; the Witch Hunter carries that mission into the field as investigator, pursuer, and enforcer.

Leader inspirations: Grand Inquisitor — Tomás de Torquemada, William Bradford, Cotton Mather, Girolamo Savonarola, and the broader archetype of the severe Puritan magistrate or doctrinal judge. Witch Hunter — Matthew Hopkins and Van Helsing: the itinerant hunter of hidden supernatural corruption.

Gauntlet takes place in an eighteenth-century powder-and-parchment world of competing provinces, chartered territories, autonomous cities, commercial possessions, and independent native nations. The six factions are institutional spheres that cross political and cultural borders: methods of exercising power rather than six countries or peoples.
