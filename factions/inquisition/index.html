Inquisition · faction guide

Turn every loss into judgment.

The Inquisition sends opposing battle cards permanently to the Graveyard, gains Conviction from those losses, and spends it to suppress Assets, hands, and Discard Piles.

At the table

How the Inquisition plays.

The Inquisition wins through attrition and denial. Even when it does not win a battle, opposing cards can become permanent losses that generate the Conviction needed for increasingly severe Purges.

Make battle costly

Condemnation sends opposing Tactics to the Graveyard instead of the Discard Pile.

Convert loss into authority

The first time each turn opposing cards enter the Graveyard during the Aftermath of a battle involving you, gain 1 Conviction. Winning is not required.

Dismantle recovery

Purges move cards, Assets, and chosen hand resources into the Graveyard, gradually exhausting the opponent’s options.

A good fit for you if

Choose Inquisition when...

  • You enjoy attrition, denial, and making resources permanently disappear.
  • You want to punish opponents for committing powerful cards or using Arcane effects.
  • You prefer a visible alternate victory created by steadily exhausting the opponent’s recoverable deck.

Signature system

The mechanics that define the faction.

01

Conviction

Gain normal Conviction from the first qualifying opposing Graveyard entry each turn, up to 4. Blasphemy also grants Conviction when an opponent plays an Arcane card for its Action effect or reveals an Arcane Gambit or Tactic.

02

Condemnation and Purge

Condemnation changes normal Tactic cleanup. Purge may be your Action in Opening or Denouement; if one phase Action is Purge, you may also take one Action in the other phase. You can never take two Actions in one phase, and the Purge Faction Action is limited to once per turn.

03

Purification

If the opponent is unable to draw to start their turn because both their Draw Pile and Discard Pile are empty, the Inquisition wins immediately.

Victory

How the Inquisition wins.

Run the Gauntlet

Removal, retaliation, and forced retreat can clear a direct path through the Territory column.

Purification

Exhaust both the opponent’s Draw Pile and Discard Pile so they are unable to draw to start their turn. Cards elsewhere do not prevent the check.

Faction Leaders

Two ways to lead.

Each Leader uses the same faction card pool and core system, but pushes it toward a different tactical identity.

Judgment, Purge, and permanent removal

Grand Inquisitor

“We judge. We purge.”

The Grand Inquisitor converts a battle victory into an immediate discounted Purge.

Leader ability

Final Judgment: once per turn during the Aftermath of a battle you won, after battle cards are cleared and effects triggered by those moves are applied, immediately Purge without taking an Action and reduce its Conviction cost by 1, to a minimum of 1. This is separate from the once-per-turn Purge Faction Action.

Defense, retaliation, and pursuit

Witch Hunter

“You ran. I followed.”

The Witch Hunter punishes failed attacks by ending the attacker’s turn and immediately pursuing them.

Leader ability

Relentless Pursuit: once per turn, at the end of the Aftermath of a battle an opponent initiated against you and lost, spend 2 Conviction to end their turn, then advance one Position. This may create a pending battle with you as attacker; no Opening or Denouement occurs first.

Archetype and setting

An institution—and a method of power.

The Inquisition represents a rigorist religious-legal institution concerned with doctrine, spiritual danger, investigation, judgment, and suppression. Its authority is recognized differently from one jurisdiction to another, and its claims can conflict sharply with local civil authority and native sovereignty. The Grand Inquisitor embodies tribunal and institutional judgment; the Witch Hunter carries that mission into the field as investigator, pursuer, and enforcer.

Leader inspirations: Grand Inquisitor — Tomás de Torquemada, William Bradford, Cotton Mather, Girolamo Savonarola, and the broader archetype of the severe Puritan magistrate or doctrinal judge. Witch Hunter — Matthew Hopkins and Van Helsing: the itinerant hunter of hidden supernatural corruption.

Gauntlet takes place in an eighteenth-century powder-and-parchment world of competing provinces, chartered territories, autonomous cities, commercial possessions, and independent native nations. The six factions are institutional spheres that cross political and cultural borders: methods of exercising power rather than six countries or peoples.
