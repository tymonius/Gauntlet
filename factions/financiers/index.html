At the table

How the Financiers play.

Financiers build an economy inside the tactical game. Every card can become an effect, Treasury value, market speculation, collateral, or a resource that helps turn contested ground into property.

Build capacity

Territories you control and the value stored in your Treasury determine how much Capital you can retain.

Invest deliberately

Capital can purchase Deeds, strengthen battles through Subsidize, or support faction cards. Spending in one area delays another.

Make ownership compound

Each Deed produces income at the start of your turn, making future purchases easier and Controlling Interest more plausible.

A good fit for you if

Choose Financiers when...

  • You enjoy engine-building and long-term economic planning.
  • You like turning the same card into several different kinds of value.
  • You want an alternate victory that opponents can see developing but cannot ignore.

Signature system

The mechanics that define the faction.

01

Capital and Treasury

Begin with 2 Capital. Place cards face up in Treasury to raise your Capital limit. Capital may exceed that limit temporarily, but excess is lost at the end of the turn.

02

Financial Capacity

After Capture and its effects, but before Draw, if Treasury value exceeds Territories controlled, you may take one Action during both Opening and Denouement. If you take both, at least one must be a Financier Faction Action.

03

Deeds and leverage

Buy Deeds independently of Territory control, risk cards through Play the Market, and spend Capital through Subsidize. Each owned Deed also produces income.

Victory

How the Financiers win.

Run the Gauntlet

Financiers can use Capital and ownership tools to support a normal territorial victory.

Controlling Interest

Own the Deed to every Territory currently in the Gauntlet and win immediately, regardless of who occupies or controls those Territories.

Faction Leaders

Two ways to lead.

Each Leader uses the same faction card pool and core system, but pushes it toward a different tactical identity.

Collateral, planned purchases, and flexible financing

Banker

“Credit closes the distance.”

The Banker uses cards in hand or Treasury as collateral to complete Deed purchases with less available Capital.

Leader ability

Line of Credit: on the first Deed purchase of your turn, one card may contribute part of the price as collateral; pay the remainder with Capital.

Offensive acquisition, occupation, and immediate control

Executive

“Take the ground. Close the deal.”

The Executive converts battlefield occupation into an immediate purchase and, when supported, an advance of the contiguous Front Line.

Leader ability

Hostile Takeover: during Denouement, after winning as the attacker that turn and becoming the occupier of an enemy Territory, take the Faction Action to buy or buy out its Deed. A successful purchase advances your Front Line by one Territory, if able; it never creates isolated control.

Archetype and setting

An institution—and a method of power.

Financiers represent banks, counting houses, chartered companies, investors, and administrators whose power comes from making paper claims and material systems shape the battlefield. The Banker controls abstract value; the Executive turns acquisition into direct control.

Visual and archetypal influences: Ledgers, strongboxes, bonds, wax seals, chartered companies, warehouses, trade goods, route maps, collateral, leverage, and early-modern commercial power.

Gauntlet’s setting is still being developed. The six factions are institutions within a broadly shared civilization, not fixed nations or isolated cultures.
