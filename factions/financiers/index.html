At the table

How the Financiers play.

Financiers build an economy inside the tactical game. Every card can become an effect, Treasury value, market speculation, collateral, or a resource that helps turn contested ground into property.

Build capacity

Territories you control and the value stored in your Treasury determine how much Capital you can retain.

Invest deliberately

Capital can purchase Deeds, strengthen battles through Subsidize, or support faction cards. Spending in one area delays another.

Make ownership compound

Each Deed produces income after Capture at the start of your turn, making future purchases easier and Controlling Interest more plausible.

A good fit for you if

Choose Financiers when...

  • You enjoy engine-building and long-term economic planning.
  • You like turning the same card into several different kinds of value.
  • You want an alternate victory that opponents can see developing but cannot ignore.

Signature system

The mechanics that define the faction.

01

Capital and Treasury

Place cards face up in Treasury during Denouement to raise your Capital limit. Capital may exceed that limit temporarily, but excess is lost at the end of the turn.

02

Financial Capacity

After Capture at the start of your turn, if Treasury value exceeds Territories controlled, you may take one Action in Opening and one in Denouement that turn. At least one Action you spend must be a Financier Faction Action.

03

Deeds and leverage

Buy or buy out Deeds during Denouement, risk cards through Play the Market, and spend Capital through Subsidize. Each owned Deed also produces income.

Victory

How the Financiers win.

Run the Gauntlet

Financiers can use Capital and ownership tools to support a normal territorial victory.

Controlling Interest

Own the Deed to every Territory currently in the Gauntlet and win immediately, regardless of who occupies or controls those Territories.

Faction Leaders

Two ways to lead.

Each Leader uses the same faction card pool and core system, but pushes it toward a different tactical identity.

Collateral, planned purchases, and flexible financing

Banker

“Credit closes the distance.”

The Banker uses a card in Hand or Treasury as collateral on the first Deed purchase or buyout of the turn.

Leader ability

Line of Credit: one collateral card contributes its value, up to half the purchase cost rounded down; pay the remainder with Capital, then put the collateral in your Discard Pile.

Offensive acquisition, occupation, and contiguous control

Executive

“Take the ground. Close the deal.”

The Executive converts battlefield occupation into an immediate opportunity to acquire the contested Territory’s Deed and, when that Territory is contiguous with the Front Line, capture it.

Leader ability

Hostile Takeover: during Denouement after winning as the attacker that turn and becoming the occupier of an enemy Territory, spend 1 Action to buy or buy out its Deed. If the purchase succeeds and that Territory is immediately beyond your Front Line, capture it; otherwise, the purchase does not change Territory control.

Archetype and setting

An institution—and a method of power.

Financiers represent banks, counting houses, investors, insurers, chartered companies, merchants, and administrators whose control of credit, property, trade, and infrastructure can rival more visibly political institutions. The Banker controls abstract value through credit, debt, collateral, and liquidity; the Executive converts capital into material systems—companies, routes, warehouses, concessions, production, and administrative control.

Leader inspirations: Banker — Alexander Hamilton and the early-modern merchant-financier tradition: money understood as credit, institutional capacity, and political power rather than mere wealth. Executive — East India Company directors, colonial company governors, and chartered-company magnates whose commercial authority could become administrative, territorial, and quasi-sovereign.

Gauntlet takes place in an eighteenth-century powder-and-parchment world of competing provinces, chartered territories, autonomous cities, commercial possessions, and independent native nations. The six factions are institutional spheres that cross political and cultural borders: methods of exercising power rather than six countries or peoples.
