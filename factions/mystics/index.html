Mystics · faction guide

Sacrifice now. Transform what remains.

At the table

How the Mystics play.

Mystics treat every card zone as part of a larger ritual economy. Cards can be spent, bound, recovered, repeated, or transformed into battle strength while the three Rites create a visible race toward Ritual.

Plan multi-turn Rites

Each Rite has a beginning cost and a later completion condition. Losses or failed position requirements can interrupt that progress.

Use sacrifice as fuel

Mystics frequently place cards in the Graveyard deliberately, then use other effects to recover or exploit them.

Grow into new powers

The first completed Rite unlocks Invocation. The second unlocks Transmutation. The third unlocks Convergence and permission to begin the Ritual of Ascension.

A good fit for you if

Choose Mystics when...

  • You enjoy sequencing, combos, and using the Graveyard as a resource.
  • You like public long-term objectives with different completion puzzles.
  • You are comfortable sacrificing immediate card value to unlock stronger systems later.

Signature system

The mechanics that define the faction.

01

The three Rites

Rite of Echoes rewards title planning, Rite of Blood rewards winning without normal battle cards, and Rite of Crossing rewards taking and holding enemy ground.

02

Invocation

After the first Rite, once per turn after an Arcane card you played, set, or chose applies its effect, move one card from your Graveyard to your Discard Pile.

03

Transmutation

After the second Rite, once per turn before battle dice, put one card from Hand in your Graveyard and add its value to your battle total.

Victory

How the Mystics win.

Run the Gauntlet

Sacrifice, recovery, and Arcane battle effects can support a direct territorial win.

Ritual of Ascension

Complete all three public Rites, then begin the Ritual by binding one Arcane card from your Hand, one from your Discard Pile, and one from your Graveyard. Initiate and win a battle while all three remain bound to complete the Ritual and win the game. Losing a battle first interrupts the Ritual.

Faction Leaders

Two ways to lead.

Each Leader uses the same faction card pool and core system, but pushes it toward a different tactical identity.

Sacrifice sequencing and card conversion

Alchemist

“Nothing is fixed. Everything can be transformed.”

The Alchemist replaces the first card deliberately sacrificed from hand through a Rite, Transmutation, or Arcane effect during the turn.

Leader ability

Materia Prima: the first qualifying sacrifice from hand on your turn draws one replacement card; during battle, draw after the battle resolves.

Ritual endurance and protective sacrifice

Spirit Walker

“The spirits remember what the living abandon.”

The Spirit Walker can sacrifice an Arcane card of sufficient value to protect a begun Rite or Ritual from the first battle-loss interruption of the turn.

Leader ability

Guardians of the Circle: the first time on your turn that a battle loss would interrupt a begun Rite or Ritual, put an Arcane card from Hand in your Graveyard whose value is at least 1 plus your completed Rites to prevent that interruption.

Archetype and setting

An institution—and a method of power.

Mystics encompass people and traditions that engage directly with the setting’s rare and disputed supernatural phenomena. The Alchemist approaches hidden forces through experiment, instruments, formulae, and an effort to make the extraordinary reproducible; the Spirit Walker belongs to culturally rooted Indigenous ceremonial traditions in which spiritual knowledge is inherited, practiced, and understood through relationships and obligations rather than experimental theory.

Leader inspirations: Alchemist — Nicolas Flamel, Paracelsus, and John Dee: the early-modern scholar working at the unstable boundary between natural philosophy and the occult. Spirit Walker — The Spirit Walker encompasses a broad range of Indigenous spiritual archetypes, from prophetic figures such as Tenskwatawa and Neolin to the darker traditions surrounding skinwalker legends.

Gauntlet takes place in an eighteenth-century powder-and-parchment world of competing provinces, chartered territories, autonomous cities, commercial possessions, and independent native nations. The six factions are institutional spheres that cross political and cultural borders: methods of exercising power rather than six countries or peoples.
