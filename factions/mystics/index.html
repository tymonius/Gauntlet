Mystics · faction guide

Sacrifice now. Transform what remains.

Mystics sacrifice cards, complete escalating Rites, recover cards from the Graveyard, convert cards into battle strength, and ultimately attempt the Ritual of Ascension.

At the table

How the Mystics play.

Mystics treat cards that other factions would consider spent or lost as ingredients in a longer ritual plan. Their power grows as Rites are completed, creating a visible race between battlefield pressure and occult progress.

Plan multi-turn Rites

Each Rite asks you to set up and sustain a different kind of achievement. Your opponent can see what you are attempting and try to interrupt it.

Use sacrifice as fuel

Mystics deliberately send cards to the Graveyard, then recover or exploit cards that would normally be out of circulation.

Grow into new powers

Each completed Rite unlocks another faction ability, so the Mystics become more flexible and dangerous as their ritual work succeeds.

A good fit for you if

Choose Mystics when...

  • You enjoy sequencing, combinations, and using the Graveyard as a resource.
  • You like public long-term objectives with different completion puzzles.
  • You are comfortable sacrificing immediate card value to unlock stronger options later.

Signature system

What makes the faction different.

01

The three Rites

Rite of Echoes rewards card-title planning, Rite of Blood rewards winning without normal battle cards, and Rite of Crossing rewards taking and holding enemy ground. You choose how to sequence them.

02

Invocation

Completing your first Rite lets Arcane play pull cards from the Graveyard back toward circulation, turning losses into future resources.

03

Transmutation and Ascension

Further Rites let you turn sacrifice into battle strength, then eventually attempt the Ritual of Ascension. The faction's power escalates as the Rites are completed.

Victory

How the Mystics win.

Run the Gauntlet

Sacrifice, recovery, and Arcane effects can support a direct territorial win while your Rites create additional pressure.

Ritual of Ascension

Complete all three Rites, bind the Arcane cards required for the Ritual, then initiate and win the battle that completes it before the Ritual is broken.

Faction Leaders

Two ways to lead.

Each Leader uses the same Rites and Arcane card pool, but one turns sacrifice into card flow while the other protects long-term ritual progress.

Sacrifice sequencing and card conversion

Alchemist

“Nothing is fixed. Everything can be transformed.”

The Alchemist replaces some deliberate sacrifices with new draws, making the Mystics' costs feel more like conversion than simple loss.

Why it matters

The Alchemist is the combination-focused Mystic. Sacrificing the right card can both pay a cost and keep your Hand moving, encouraging chains of effects rather than simple conservation.

Ritual endurance and protective sacrifice

Spirit Walker

“The spirits remember what the living abandon.”

The Spirit Walker can protect ritual progress from a battle loss by sacrificing an Arcane card, making long-term Rites harder to disrupt.

Why it matters

The Spirit Walker is the steadier Mystic. Keeping the right Arcane card in reserve lets you take battlefield risks without surrendering all the work invested in a Rite.

Archetype and setting

An institution—and a method of power.

Mystics encompass people and traditions that engage directly with the setting's rare and disputed supernatural phenomena. The Alchemist approaches hidden forces through experiment, instruments, formulae, and an effort to make the extraordinary reproducible; the Spirit Walker belongs to culturally rooted Indigenous ceremonial traditions in which spiritual knowledge is inherited, practiced, and understood through relationships and obligations rather than experimental theory.

Leader inspirations: Alchemist — Nicolas Flamel, Paracelsus, and John Dee: the early-modern scholar working at the unstable boundary between natural philosophy and the occult. Spirit Walker — The Spirit Walker encompasses a broad range of Indigenous spiritual archetypes, from prophetic figures such as Tenskwatawa and Neolin to the darker traditions surrounding skinwalker legends.

Gauntlet takes place in an eighteenth-century powder-and-parchment world of competing provinces, chartered territories, autonomous cities, commercial possessions, and independent native nations. The six factions are institutional spheres that cross political and cultural borders: methods of exercising power rather than six countries or peoples.
