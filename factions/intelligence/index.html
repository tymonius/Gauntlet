At the table

How Intelligence plays.

Intelligence rewards planning several turns ahead. Your opponent can see that you are pursuing a Mission, but not what it requires, while Intel lets you challenge some of the hidden information that normally defines Gauntlet's battles.

Work toward hidden objectives

A Mission gives you a private goal to pursue while you move, fight, and use cards normally. The opponent knows you are working toward something, but not exactly what.

Control information

Intel lets you inspect or disrupt hidden battle choices, making uncertainty itself a resource you can manage.

Prepare the operation

Completed Missions build Operation Progress toward a Special Operation, so each smaller objective can contribute to a secret endgame.

A good fit for you if

Choose Intelligence when...

  • You enjoy hidden objectives and delayed payoffs.
  • You want to interfere with the opponent's private battle decisions.
  • You like coordinating information, resources, and board state toward one decisive operation.

Signature system

What makes the faction different.

01

Missions

Missions turn ordinary movement, battles, and card play into hidden objectives. Completing them rewards both Intel and Operation Progress.

02

Intel

Intel buys information and interference. It can reveal what the opponent hoped to keep hidden or force a battle plan to change.

03

Special Operations

Once enough Mission progress is built, a Special Operation becomes your hidden final objective and your faction's additional route to victory.

Victory

How Intelligence wins.

Run the Gauntlet

Information advantages and Mission rewards can support the normal territorial route while keeping the opponent uncertain about your real priorities.

Special Operation

Build enough Operation Progress to make a Special Operation available, then complete its hidden requirement and pay its Intel cost to win.

Faction Leaders

Two ways to lead.

Each Leader uses the same Missions and Intel, but one keeps field plans from being disrupted while the other accelerates the Mission cycle itself.

Territory effects, reconnaissance, and field operations

Ranger

“Know the land before the battle begins.”

The Ranger uses Intel to neutralize troublesome Territory effects, keeping important Missions and attacks on course when the terrain would otherwise interfere.

Why it matters

The Ranger is the field operator: terrain and positional complications are less likely to derail a plan. He rewards Missions that align with movement and battlefield objectives.

Mission tempo and operational coordination

Spymaster

“Information never rests. Momentum is the weapon.”

The Spymaster can roll from one completed Mission straight into the next, keeping Operation Progress moving without losing tempo.

Why it matters

The Spymaster rewards having the next objective ready before the current one ends. Chaining Missions brings the Special Operation within reach faster.

Archetype and setting

An institution—and a method of power.

Intelligence represents scouts, surveyors, agents, informants, couriers, analysts, and the networks that turn information into action across political and institutional boundaries. The Ranger works in the field through reconnaissance, terrain knowledge, exploration, and irregular operations; the Spymaster directs covert networks through ciphers, intercepted correspondence, informants, dead drops, and patient operational planning.

Leader inspirations: Ranger — Robert Rogers and Lewis & Clark, combining frontier irregular warfare with disciplined reconnaissance, exploration, surveying, and field observation. Spymaster — Robert Townsend and the Culper Ring: discreet intelligence organizers whose effectiveness depends on networks and concealment rather than conspicuous personal action.

Gauntlet takes place in an eighteenth-century powder-and-parchment world of competing provinces, chartered territories, autonomous cities, commercial possessions, and independent native nations. The six factions are institutional spheres that cross political and cultural borders: methods of exercising power rather than six countries or peoples.
