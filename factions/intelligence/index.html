At the table

How Intelligence plays.

Intelligence rewards planning several turns ahead. Your opponent can see that a Mission exists, but not what it requires; meanwhile Intel lets you look into the hidden decisions that normally define Gauntlet’s battles.

Work toward hidden objectives

Begin one face-down Mission, satisfy its requirement through normal play, then take 1 Action during a later Denouement to complete it.

Control information

Surveillance lets you inspect a face-down opposing battle card. Interference can remove that choice and force a replacement.

Prepare the operation

Completed Missions produce both Intel and Operation Progress, opening the window for a decisive Special Operation.

A good fit for you if

Choose Intelligence when...

  • You enjoy hidden objectives and delayed payoffs.
  • You want to interfere with the opponent’s private battle decisions.
  • You like coordinating information, resources, and board state toward one decisive operation.

Signature system

The mechanics that define the faction.

01

Missions

Six Intelligence cards carry Mission requirements. Completed normal Missions grant 1 Operation Progress and Intel equal to the card’s value.

02

Surveillance and Interference

Spend Intel to look at an opponent’s hidden battle choice, then spend more to remove it from the battle before play continues.

03

Special Operations

Once Operation Progress exceeds the opponent’s controlled Territories, prepare a face-down Special Operation, satisfy its requirement, and pay its Intel cost to win.

Victory

How Intelligence wins.

Run the Gauntlet

Information advantages and Mission rewards can support the normal territorial route.

Special Operation

Complete enough Missions to make an operation ready, keep that readiness while the opponent contests Territory control, satisfy the hidden requirement, and pay the final Intel cost.

Faction Leaders

Two ways to lead.

Each Leader uses the same faction card pool and core system, but pushes it toward a different tactical identity.

Territory effects, reconnaissance, and field operations

Ranger

“Know the land before the battle begins.”

The Ranger spends Intel to prevent a printed Territory effect from disrupting movement or an operation for the rest of the turn.

Leader ability

Fieldcraft: once per turn, spend 1 Intel to ignore a Territory’s printed effect when it would affect you, your movement, or a battle involving you.

Mission tempo and operational coordination

Spymaster

“Information never rests. Momentum is the weapon.”

The Spymaster keeps the mission cycle moving, beginning another normal Mission as soon as one is completed.

Leader ability

Mission Control: once per turn, after completing a normal Mission, immediately start another eligible Mission from Hand without spending an Action. It cannot complete that turn and cannot be a Special Operation.

Archetype and setting

An institution—and a method of power.

Intelligence represents scouts, surveyors, agents, informants, couriers, analysts, and the networks that turn information into action across political and institutional boundaries. The Ranger works in the field through reconnaissance, terrain knowledge, exploration, and irregular operations; the Spymaster directs covert networks through ciphers, intercepted correspondence, informants, dead drops, and patient operational planning.

Leader inspirations: Ranger — Robert Rogers and Lewis & Clark, combining frontier irregular warfare with disciplined reconnaissance, exploration, surveying, and field observation. Spymaster — Robert Townsend and the Culper Ring: discreet intelligence organizers whose effectiveness depends on networks and concealment rather than conspicuous personal action.

Gauntlet takes place in an eighteenth-century powder-and-parchment world of competing provinces, chartered territories, autonomous cities, commercial possessions, and independent native nations. The six factions are institutional spheres that cross political and cultural borders: methods of exercising power rather than six countries or peoples.
