Step 1 · Know the goal

Push through six Territories and break the opponent's line.

Gauntlet is a two-player tactical card game played on a battlefield made from six Territory cards—three from each player.

Advance. Win battles. Hold the ground you take.

Each player begins at their own end of the Gauntlet. Each turn, capture if eligible, draw one card, take up to one Action before or after movement, and choose whether to Advance, Hold, or Fall Back. If you Advance into your opponent's position, you fight a battle.

Winning an attack pushes the defender back and leaves you occupying their ground. Hold that advance until a later Capture step and the Territory can become yours. Keep pressing forward until you capture the Territory at your opponent's end or drive them beyond the Gauntlet and defeat their Last Stand.

Five factions also have an additional way to win, but every faction can win by pushing through the battlefield.

What each player needs

  • One faction and one Leader
  • One recommended 30-card starter Deck
  • Three different Territories
  • One Player Token and one six-sided die
  • Any trackers, references, or supplemental cards used by that faction

The Deckbuilder print package includes the chosen Leader, playable cards, Territories, strategy summary, and required printable faction components. Tokens and dice are supplied separately.

Two players, one shared guide. Each player needs their own game package, but both players can learn from this page together. Keep the Rulebook or Rules Arbiter nearby for exact timing; neither player needs to memorize everything before starting.

Step 3 · Learn the basics · First Game Introduction

The first game, in six steps.

This is enough to begin playing. Use the browser Rulebook or Rules Arbiter for exact timing, unusual interactions, and your faction's special procedures.

The core loop

Capture. Draw. Act. Move.

At the start of your turn, first capture any enemy ground you have held long enough. Then draw one card. You may take one Action either before or after movement. During movement, choose to Advance, Hold, or Fall Back. If an Advance moves you into your opponent, fight immediately. At the end of the turn, discard down to three cards.

01

Set up the Gauntlet

  1. Each player takes a faction, Leader, recommended Deck, three Territories, Player Token, and die.
  2. Prepare any faction or Leader setup that changes the Deck, then shuffle it.
  3. Draw four cards, discard one face up, and keep three as your opening Hand.
  4. After seeing that Hand, secretly arrange your three Territories in a line.
  5. Join both lines into one six-Territory Gauntlet, reveal them, place each token at its own end, and roll for first player.
02

Take one Action

On most turns you get one Action. Spend it either before you move or after movement is finished.

Common Actions include playing a card from Hand for its Action effect, banking a card as an Asset, using a faction or Leader option marked 1 Action, or discarding one of your Assets.

The rulebook calls the before-movement Action phase Opening and the after-movement Action phase Denouement. If you do not know what you want to do yet, simply save your Action until after movement.

03

Move

During normal movement, choose one:

  • Advance: move one position toward the opponent's end.
  • Hold: stay where you are.
  • Fall Back: move one position toward your own end.

If you Advance into your opponent's position, stop moving and begin a battle.

04

Fight a battle

A normal battle gives each player two card choices and one die roll:

  1. Gambit: you may set one eligible card from your Hand face down. Gambits are costly—they normally go to the Graveyard after battle.
  2. Reserve: set your Hand aside and draw three cards into a private Reserve.
  3. Reveal and resolve the Gambits.
  4. Tactic: you may choose one eligible card from your Reserve face down, then reveal and resolve the Tactics.
  5. Apply active effects, roll, and compare battle totals. The higher total wins. A defender normally has Defensive Edge when defending a Territory they control or making a Last Stand, and Defensive Edge wins a tied total. If a tie is still unresolved, make a separate unmodified Tiebreak Roll. The loser retreats.

Tactics and unused Reserve cards normally go to the Discard Pile, where they can be drawn again later. Faction and Territory effects may add or change steps; for example, Diplomat Terms happen before Gambits.

05

Capture what you hold

Winning an attack on an enemy-controlled Territory does not flip it immediately. The defender retreats and the attacker stays there as the occupier.

If your token is still on or beyond the next enemy Territory when one of your later Capture steps begins, capture that Territory and rotate it to face you. Your captured line—your Front Line—always grows outward from your own end, normally one Territory at a time.

06

Run the Gauntlet

The simplest victory is to capture the Territory at your opponent's end.

You can also win by pushing the opponent beyond their end of the Gauntlet. After that has happened, another legal Advance can force a Last Stand; win that battle and you win the game.

Five factions have an additional victory route. Learn that route only after choosing the faction—you never need it to understand the shared game.

Do not memorize the vocabulary. For a first game, remember the sequence: capture, draw, act, move; when you collide, choose a Gambit and Tactic, roll, and push the loser back; hold enemy ground until you can capture it.
