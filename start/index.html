Step 1 · Understand

A campaign fought across six territories.

Gauntlet is a two-player tactical card-and-territory game. Each player brings a faction, leader, Deck, and three-Territory line.

Advance, fight, and hold what you take.

The players join their Territory lines into one six-position battlefield called the Gauntlet. They advance toward one another, commit hidden cards in battle, develop Assets, occupy opposing ground, and advance a contiguous Front Line through Capture.

There are two normal ways to run the Gauntlet and win immediately: capture the Territory at your opponent's end or force your opponent to make a Last Stand and win the resulting battle. A Last Stand can occur without first capturing the final Territory. Five factions also have an additional victory route tied to their own system.

What each player needs

  • One faction and one Leader
  • One 30-card recommended starter Deck
  • Three different Territories
  • One Player Token and one six-sided die
  • Any trackers, references, or supplemental cards used by that faction

The Deckbuilder print package includes the chosen Leader, playable cards, Territories, strategy summary, and required printable faction components. Tokens and dice are supplied separately.

You need another player. Each player should complete this page separately and print a different starter Deck. You can learn together; neither player needs to act as a permanent teacher.

Step 3 · Learn

Follow the first game in play order.

This teaches the ordinary flow. Keep the browser Rulebook and Rules Arbiter available for exact timing, faction procedures, and unusual interactions.

The shape of play

One Action. Two opportunities. Every advance matters.

Every turn follows Capture → Draw → Opening → Movement → Denouement → Cleanup. You normally take one Action during either Opening or Denouement. Your faction changes what you can build toward, but both players share this turn and battle structure.

01

Cards and zones

Your Hand is private. Your Draw Pile supplies cards; your Discard Pile can be recycled; your Graveyard normally cannot. Face-up Assets remain in your Asset Bank.

A battle creates three temporary areas: a Gambit set from Hand, a three-card private Reserve drawn from the Draw Pile, and a Tactic chosen from that Reserve.

02

Setup

  1. Prepare faction and Leader components that add cards to or remove cards from the Deck.
  2. Shuffle the remaining Deck to form the Draw Pile.
  3. Draw four cards, discard one face up, and keep three as your opening Hand.
  4. With that Hand and opening discard known, secretly arrange your three Territories.
  5. Join both Territory lines and reveal the six-Territory Gauntlet.
  6. Place your Player Token on the Territory at your own end. This is not movement and does not count as entering.
  7. After both players finish opening selection and Territory arrangement, roll for first player; reroll ties.
03

Your turn

  1. Capture: advance your Front Line if your Position satisfies the Capture rules.
  2. Draw: draw one card.
  3. Opening: take your Action now, or save it.
  4. Movement: Advance, Hold, or Fall Back; resolve any battle caused by movement immediately.
  5. Denouement: take your Action now if you did not take it during Opening.
  6. Cleanup: resolve end-of-turn effects, discard down to the Hand limit, and pass the active-player marker.
04

Actions and Assets

An Action may play a card for its Action effect, bank a card from Hand using its inherent Bank Action if it has an Asset effect, take a legal Faction Feature marked 1 Action, or discard one of your Assets.

An additional Action does not by itself permit two Actions in one phase.

05

Movement

During normal Movement, choose Advance, Hold, or Fall Back. Entering the opponent's Position begins Onset and ends that Movement sequence.

Fall Back is ordinary Movement. A player who loses a battle retreats. A player who leaves an Onset or battle sequence without losing withdraws.

06

Battle sequence

  1. Begin Onset; if a Diplomat is involved, resolve Terms first. Accepted Terms end the sequence before a battle is fought.
  2. Resolve the remaining Onset effects.
  3. Each player may set one eligible Hand card as a face-down Gambit.
  4. Each player normally draws three cards into a private Reserve.
  5. Reveal and resolve Gambits.
  6. Each player may normally choose one eligible Reserve card as a face-down Tactic.
  7. Reveal and resolve Tactics.
  8. Apply active effects, roll, and compare battle totals; then resolve the Aftermath.

If the defender has Defensive Edge, the defender wins tied battle totals. If a tie remains unresolved, make a separate unmodified Tiebreak Roll; higher roll wins and further ties are rerolled.

07

Aftermath

Apply the result, retreat the loser, establish Occupation when applicable, resolve Aftermath effects, and clear battle cards to their normal destinations.

When an attacker wins on an opposing controlled Territory, the defender retreats and the attacker remains as the occupier. Control does not change yet.

08

Front Line and Capture

If your token remains on or beyond the next opposing Territory immediately beyond your Front Line until a later Capture step, that Territory joins your Front Line.

Control remains contiguous from your own end, Position may extend beyond control, and normal Capture advances the Front Line by at most one Territory.

09

Run the Gauntlet

You win immediately by either capturing the Territory at your opponent's end or forcing your opponent to make a Last Stand and winning the resulting battle.

A separate legal movement sequence can force the opponent to make a Last Stand after the opponent is forced beyond their end. You do not need to capture the final Territory first.
