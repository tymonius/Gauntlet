# Gauntlet v0.7.2 — The Chief Justice

**Release candidate changelog draft · publication date pending**

Gauntlet v0.7.2 is primarily a **rules-access and new-player-experience release**. Its headlining change is a substantially rebuilt **Rules Arbiter**, now presented to players as the **Chief Justice**. The second major change is a much less intimidating path into the game: a streamlined **Start Playing** flow and a modular rules library that replaces the old all-in-one 100-page Rulebook as the normal way to learn and reference Gauntlet.

The gameplay changes are deliberately narrower: **Intelligence receives a stronger recurring Intel economy and Operational Capacity**, three Territories are rebalanced, the twelve starter Territory packages are re-evaluated, and several existing rules/card texts are clarified around Action timing, Capture, Front Line advancement, battle commitment order, and Run the Gauntlet.

This is the canonical player-facing changelog from **v0.7.1 → v0.7.2**. Repository-only refactors, archival work, CI maintenance, and digital-engine implementation of already-published rules are intentionally omitted unless they change the player-facing product or release reliability.

## At a glance

- **The Rules Arbiter is now the Chief Justice** in the player-facing experience, with a major accuracy, retrieval, conversation-continuity, clarification, source-selection, and ruling-presentation overhaul.
- **v0.7.2 is gated on Chief Justice quality.** The final release must pass historical regressions and fresh blind questions against the final v0.7.2 rules corpus before publication.
- **Start Playing has been rebuilt for first-time players** around the normal battlefield loop rather than a compressed version of the full Rulebook.
- **The old 100-page all-in-one Rulebook is retired as the primary learning document.** It is replaced by a Player's Guide, six Faction Guides, and Complete Rules.
- **The Browser Rulebook now hosts the full modular rules set**, and each document has its own matching printable half-letter booklet.
- **Intelligence now gains Intel at the start of each turn equal to Operation Progress.**
- **Operational Capacity** lets Intelligence use a qualifying Mission or Special Operation Action during Denouement after spending the normal Action during Opening.
- **Supply Depot, Smuggler's Run, and Quicksand are rebalanced.**
- **Ten of the twelve starter Territory packages change**, with the full twelve-package set re-evaluated for competitive strength and Leader expression.
- **Action timing is made explicit on seven phase-restricted cards**, using visible “Play only during …” wording.
- **Capture is distinguished from Advance Front Line**, correcting six cards/abilities/Proposal effects that previously used Front Line language where direct Capture was intended.
- **Run the Gauntlet's territorial route is now taught as controlling the entire Gauntlet**, with the final opposing Territory as the completion trigger rather than a standalone shortcut objective.
- **Normal battle commitment order is explicitly restored:** attacker commits first, then defender, for both Gambits and Tactics; commitments are normally face down.
- The playable pool remains **142 cards**, with **25 Territories, six factions, and twelve Leaders**. v0.7.2 does not add or retire card or Territory titles.

## The Chief Justice

The Rules Arbiter remains the underlying system; **Chief Justice** is the player-facing character and table authority.

The v0.7.2 development cycle substantially rebuilds the Arbiter around the kinds of failures that matter during an actual game rather than around isolated answer demonstrations.

### Player-facing identity

The interface now consistently presents:

**GAUNTLET RULES ARBITER**  
**Chief Justice**

The launcher and major calls to action use **Ask the Chief Justice**, while technical metadata, APIs, and internal tooling continue to use Rules Arbiter terminology.

The Chief Justice also receives a dedicated visual identity across the standalone Rules Arbiter, floating assistant, and homepage.

### Better rulings

The release-cycle hardening focuses on:

- ruling first, with enough explanation to settle the table question without dumping unrelated rules;
- selecting the most directly governing authority rather than merely retrieving semantically nearby text;
- preserving player-stated game state rather than replacing it with an unstated hypothetical;
- carrying the active card, ability, Territory, Mission, or other object naturally through short follow-up questions;
- preserving possessives and object ownership/control correctly through multi-object interactions;
- distinguishing literal printed shorthand from broader prose that happens to describe a similar result;
- handling negative-form questions with consistent yes/no polarity;
- applying named-card or optional exceptions only when the player actually invokes them;
- asking for clarification when the question is genuinely ambiguous, but not when the answer is universal and the missing detail cannot change it;
- keeping unsupported or out-of-scope questions from turning into confident invented rulings; and
- keeping internal retrieval/source markers out of player-facing answers while still presenting useful governing sources.

The no-model/source-lookup fallback path is also preserved so a temporary model-service failure does not turn the Rules Arbiter into an authoritative-sounding guesser.

### Release quality gate

The Chief Justice is the release gate for v0.7.2, not simply a website redesign.

Before publication, the final v0.7.2 build must:

- preserve the accumulated fixed-failure regression set;
- survive fresh blind questions that were not used to construct its fixes;
- cover core rules, all six factions, all twelve Leaders, named-card interactions, combined-rule questions, follow-ups, ambiguous questions, unsupported cases, and ordinary player language;
- avoid recurring plausible-but-wrong rulings even if the aggregate score is otherwise high; and
- be rerun against the **actual final v0.7.2 corpus**, rather than relying only on certification against the frozen v0.7.1 corpus used during development.

## A much easier first game

v0.7.2 deliberately separates **learning the game** from **looking up every technical rule**.

### Start Playing

The Start Playing page is rewritten around a first-time player's mental model:

**move → fight → push back → hold ground → capture → win**

Instead of asking a new player to absorb the vocabulary and exceptions of the full rules corpus, it teaches five practical shared-game steps:

1. setup;
2. the turn;
3. movement;
4. battle; and
5. taking ground and winning.

Faction selection is intentionally shallow at first: a short play-identity description and a **Learning Curve** indicator explain what kind of extra rules load the faction adds, not how strong or difficult the player is expected to be.

After the shared game is taught, **Learn Your Faction** dynamically teaches only the selected faction and gives Leader-specific first-game priorities for all twelve Leaders. Heavier systems such as Financier finance or Mystic Rites use progressive disclosure instead of being prerequisites to starting the game.

The old First Game Introduction confirmation gate is removed. New players can use an automatically supplied recommended starter Deck and begin playing without treating custom deck construction or the complete technical rules as required reading.

### Modular rules instead of one textbook

The v0.7.1 Rulebook was a single roughly 100-page document trying to serve four jobs at once: teach the game, teach all six factions, explain every Leader, and act as the technical reference.

v0.7.2 splits those jobs:

- **Player's Guide** — teaches the shared game and first-game flow;
- **Military Guide**
- **Diplomats Guide**
- **Financiers Guide**
- **Intelligence Guide**
- **Mystics Guide**
- **Inquisition Guide**
- **Complete Rules** — the technical reference for exact procedures, timing, definitions, and edge cases.

The Complete Rules are therefore roughly half the size of the old monolithic Rulebook even though the total v0.7.2 documentation set is substantially richer.

### Browser Rulebook and printable booklets

The Browser Rulebook is the common publication surface for the modular rules set. Players can switch among the Player's Guide, six Faction Guides, and Complete Rules without moving between unrelated readers.

Each document also has its own dedicated **Printable booklet**. The printable control follows the document currently displayed instead of always opening the old v0.7.1 Rulebook.

The eight booklets use the established Gauntlet publication language rather than simply printing the browser shell:

- half-letter reader pages and saddle-stitch Letter imposition;
- heritage cover, contents, typography, running furniture, callouts, tables, and faction identity;
- faction symbols and low-opacity faction watermarks;
- Leader woodcuts fitted to the actual visible art rather than source-image whitespace;
- restored Leader flavor quotes;
- P22 Declaration Pro for faction and Leader motto treatment;
- faction-colored Player's Guide callout boxes;
- print-safe footer/page-number placement; and
- designed hero-art filler pages placed strategically rather than stacked at the end.

When padding is necessary, the inside front cover is preferred first; additional filler pages are distributed through appropriate section boundaries, and filler pages are not placed back to back.

### At-the-table tools

The Player's Guide now includes an **At the Table: Digital Tools** page with scannable QR codes and short URLs for:

- Ask the Chief Justice;
- Card Reference;
- Full Rules;
- Deckbuilder;
- Start Playing; and
- Start a Playtest.

## Intelligence economy and Operational Capacity

Intelligence receives the principal faction-balance change in v0.7.2.

### Recurring Intel

At the start of an Intelligence player's turn:

> **Gain Intel equal to your Operation Progress.**

Operation Progress remains the number of **normal Missions completed**. It is progression/bookkeeping, not a spendable resource.

Completing a normal Mission still:

- increases Operation Progress by 1; and
- immediately grants Intel equal to that Mission card's value.

The increased Operation Progress then improves recurring Intel generation on later turns.

### Operational Capacity

Intelligence gains a new automatic Faction Feature:

**Operational Capacity**

If the Intelligence player used their normal Action during Opening, they may still spend 1 Action during Denouement to:

- Start a Mission;
- Complete a Mission;
- Start a Special Operation; or
- Complete a Special Operation.

This does **not** create a free or unrestricted second Action. The one-Action-per-phase limit remains in place, and **Abort Mission does not qualify** for Operational Capacity.

The Intelligence Guide and Mission Reference are rewritten around this model so Operation Progress is taught as accumulated completed-Mission momentum that generates future Intel rather than as another resource pool.

## Territory rebalance

The Territory pool remains 25 cards, but three Territory effects change.

### Quicksand

Previously, Quicksand limited a player who **began Movement there** to one Position of Advance/Fall Back and prevented movement increases.

It now additionally says:

> **When a player enters this Territory, their movement ends.**

The existing one-Position restriction still applies to a player who begins Movement there, and Retreat remains unaffected.

### Supply Depot

Supply Depot changes from an unconditional start-of-turn **+1 Card** to optional filtering:

> If Supply Depot's controller starts their turn here, they may **draw 1 card, then discard 1 card**.

### Smuggler's Run

Smuggler's Run is made less location-dependent after the stash is established.

- Stashing is limited to **once per turn**.
- The controller still must be there to stash a Hand card beneath it.
- Once stashed, the card may be used for its Action effect or as a Gambit **while the player controls Smuggler's Run**, even after moving away.
- The normal start-of-turn recovery remains available while controlling it.
- Losing control still discards the stash.

The final v0.7.2 packages are:

- **Ambassador — Open Channels:** Refuge / Supply Depot / Command Tent
- **Senator — Procedure Endures:** Refuge / Garrison / Fortified Pass
- **Banker — Sound Investment:** Supply Depot / Ruined Storehouse / Arena: Spoils of War
- **Executive — Hostile Expansion:** Smuggler's Run / King's Road / Arena: No Quarter
- **Ranger — Field Operations:** Quicksand / Difficult Terrain / Poisonous Gas
- **Spymaster — Mission Network:** Refuge / Ruined Storehouse / Field Hospital
- **Alchemist — First Principles:** Poisonous Gas / Old Battlefield / Ruined Storehouse

## Action timing cleanup

v0.7.2 makes phase restrictions visible in the card text rather than leaving players to infer them from when the effect could be useful.

The following Action effects now explicitly use **Play only during Opening**:

- Advance Guard;
- Forced March;
- Invasion; and
- Fate's Toll.

The following now explicitly use **Play only during Denouement**:

- Foreclosure; and
- Give Chase.

**Battlefield Promotion** already used the correct visible Denouement wording; v0.7.2 adds matching machine-readable timing metadata for consistency.

This does change the legal timing of cards whose v0.7.1 wording did not expressly restrict the phase: the visible v0.7.2 text is authoritative.

## Capture versus Advance Front Line

v0.7.2 makes an important semantic distinction explicit:

- **Capture** changes control of the specified Territory, if that capture is legal.
- **Advance Front Line** changes control of the **next opposing Territory immediately beyond the player's Front Line**.

Those are not interchangeable instructions.

The following effects are corrected from Front Line advancement language to direct Capture where the intended object is the Territory being occupied/contested:

- **Assimilation** — Asset and Gambit/Tactic effects;
- **Foreclosure** — Gambit/Tactic effect;
- **Commandant — Fortify**;
- **Executive — Hostile Takeover**;
- **Diplomatic Recognition** — Accepted and Refused effects; and
- **Shock and Awe — Consolidate**.

The Foreclosure **Action** remains a genuine Front Line advance because it specifically targets the next opposing Territory beyond the Front Line.

## Run the Gauntlet victory language

The territorial Run the Gauntlet route is now described as:

> **Control the entire Gauntlet.**

This is a teaching/rules-concept clarification rather than a new victory condition.

Capture remains contiguous from a player's own end. The opponent-end Territory therefore cannot be captured as an isolated shortcut; it is necessarily the final opposing Territory whose Capture completes control of all six Territories.

The other normal route remains independent:

> Force the opponent to make a **Last Stand**, then win the resulting Last Stand battle.

A Last Stand does not require first controlling the opponent's final Territory.

## Battle commitment order restored explicitly

The normal battle commitment sequence is stated directly again:

- attacker sets a Gambit or passes first;
- defender then sets a Gambit or passes;
- after Reserves are formed, attacker chooses a Tactic or passes first;
- defender then chooses a Tactic or passes; and
- Gambits and Tactics are normally committed **face down** unless a specific rule or effect says otherwise.

This restores existing intended behavior that had been omitted during earlier rules consolidation; it is not a new v0.7.2 balance change.

Effects such as Watchtower, Fog of War, Neutral Observers, and Intelligence Surveillance continue to modify or depend on that default procedure as printed.

## Rules clarity and authority cleanup

A large part of the v0.7.2 rules work is not new gameplay but making the current rules complete, directly findable, and publishable without maintaining hidden second authorities.

### Complete current authority

Current shared and faction procedures are normalized into the canonical current-game authority, including:

- setup and Deck construction;
- card-zone identity and information rules;
- Actions, Assets, movement, battle commitments, Reserve, withdrawal, and bound cards;
- reveal-stage interference, copied/repeated effects, shorthand, and battle-ending-without-a-winner procedure;
- complete procedures for all six faction systems.

The Complete Rules can therefore be generated as a direct technical projection of current authority rather than copying rules from teaching prose.

### Restored shared baseline

Rules consolidation also restored current shared procedures that had become implicit or incomplete in structured authority, including:

- battle totals and ordinary dice;
- Advantage/Disadvantage;
- normal battle results and ordered Aftermath;
- Draw/Discard recycling;
- Action limits and Cleanup;
- movement boundaries and Counterattacks;
- simultaneous-effect timing;
- reveal, negation, replacement, and revised-choice rules; and
- Overlay defaults.

These are restorations of already-current play, not new mechanics.

### Editorial clarifications

The current rules/publication surfaces also receive smaller cleanup passes, including:

- removing redundant two-player phrases such as “battle involving you” where the two-player rules already make the scope unambiguous;
- showing the actual compact shorthand alongside its explanations in Complete Rules;
- removing redundant plural glossary/index redirects where a plural entry only said “See [singular]”; and
- adopting a natural-English editorial policy so defined terms are used precisely without turning rules prose into code-like jargon.

Unless specifically changed above, v0.7.2 retains the v0.7.1 game:

- **142 playable cards** — 52 Neutral and 15 in each faction;
- **25 Territories**, including the existing Arenas;
- **six factions** and **twelve Leaders**;
- the v0.7.1 six-Rite Mystics selection system;
- the existing Diplomat Proposal set and six-Proposal Peace Treaty threshold;
- the shared turn structure and Onset → Gambits → Reserves → Tactics → Outcome → Aftermath battle framework;

- the established universal Run-the-Gauntlet and faction-specific victory routes, except for the clarifications and Intelligence changes expressly listed above.
