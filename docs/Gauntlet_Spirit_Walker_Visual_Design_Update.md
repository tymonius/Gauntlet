# Gauntlet Spirit Walker Visual Design Update

**Status:** Active production-facing character-design supplement.  
**Purpose:** Lock the revised visual direction for the Spirit Walker and supersede older portrait guidance where it conflicts with this document.

This document governs the Spirit Walker's appearance in illustrations, leader portraits, miniatures, promotional art, and future derived assets. It supplements the [Leader Design Bible](Gauntlet_v0.6_Leader_Design_Bible.md) and the [Illustration Art Direction](Gauntlet_Illustration_Art_Direction.md).

The Spirit Walker's existing portrait remains useful as an earlier concept, but it is no longer the controlling visual reference. The revised direction established during the July 2026 illustration review is closer to the intended character and should govern future work.

---

## 1. Core visual statement

> **A young, antlered eastern-woodland ritual shapeshifter: heavily painted, minimally clothed, predatory, and unmistakably supernatural while remaining recognizably human.**

The Spirit Walker should not read as an elderly medicine man, a generic fantasy shaman, or a conventional Plains warrior. He should feel like a dangerous person who has moved too close to the unseen world and may no longer return from it unchanged.

His menace comes from ritual purpose, body language, and supernatural proximity—not from monstrous anatomy or conventional villain costume.

---

## 2. Character design

### Age and physicality

Use:

- a young adult man;
- a lean, athletic, human build;
- long dark hair, loose or roughly bound;
- alert, intense eyes;
- poised, controlled movement rather than frailty or elder wisdom;
- human proportions, even when the pose or silhouette feels uncanny.

Avoid:

- elderly sage styling;
- heavily weathered medicine-man presentation;
- giant scale or inhuman limb proportions;
- corpse-like gauntness;
- literal transformation into a creature.

### Antlered silhouette

Antlers are the Spirit Walker's defining silhouette and must remain visible and legible.

Use:

- a large branching antler crown or headdress;
- asymmetrical natural forms where practical;
- raw bindings, hide, sinew, wood, bone, and small ritual attachments;
- a design that feels assembled through inherited practice rather than manufactured as fantasy armor.

The antlers should create an immediate supernatural read without becoming a giant ornamental helmet.

Avoid:

- feather warbonnets;
- crown-like metal fantasy headpieces;
- decorative antlers added only as a generic druid symbol;
- excessive height or weight that makes the character look nonhuman.

### Paint and exposed body

Body paint should carry more of the design than clothing.

Use:

- extensive chalky white paint across the face, torso, arms, and legs;
- dark red and charcoal markings;
- hand-applied, imperfect, asymmetrical patterns;
- paint that follows joints, ribs, tendons, or ritual pathways;
- enough exposed skin for the painted body to remain a primary visual element.

The paint should feel ceremonial, unsettling, and specific to a fictional Gauntlet tradition. It should not reproduce identifiable sacred marks from a living culture.

### Clothing and materials

The Spirit Walker should wear relatively little clothing compared with the other leaders.

Use:

- hide, fur, wool, bark fiber, leather, and rough woven cloth;
- a short mantle, waist wrap, layered skirt, or practical ritual coverings;
- wrapped forearms, calves, or feet;
- bone, wood, teeth, small antler pieces, cords, and inherited ritual objects;
- materials that belong to a damp eastern woodland and mountain environment.

Avoid:

- full ceremonial robes;
- elaborate Plains-style regalia;
- dense beadwork used as generic indigenous shorthand;
- large feather displays;
- a fully clothed fantasy-druid silhouette;
- clothing that hides most of the body paint.

---

## 3. Cultural and regional read

The environmental and material language should lean **Appalachian and eastern woodland**, filtered through Gauntlet's fictional setting.

Prefer:

- dense deciduous forest;
- mountain ridges and narrow hollows;
- damp earth, exposed roots, moss, fog, and smoke;
- rough shelters, ritual clearings, carved stakes, stone rings, and fire bowls;
- dark green, wet brown, charcoal, bone white, dried blood red, and ember orange.

Avoid:

- open Plains landscapes;
- tipi or buffalo-hunt shorthand;
- southwestern desert imagery;
- generic pan-indigenous scenery;
- direct replication of any one real living culture.

The Spirit Walker may evoke the unease associated with skinwalker or shapeshifter folklore, but he is a fictional Gauntlet character rather than a direct representation of Navajo belief or ritual.

---

## 4. Personality and mood

The Spirit Walker should feel:

- younger;
- sinister;
- watchful;
- trance-like;
- predatory;
- disciplined rather than wild;
- spiritually dangerous rather than benevolent;
- capable of protection, sacrifice, and ritual endurance without reading as comforting or priestly.

The viewer should sense that he is not merely performing a rite. He is negotiating with—or becoming partially aligned with—something that should not be present.

Avoid the stock reads of:

- wise healer;
- kindly medicine man;
- nature guardian;
- theatrical stage shaman;
- berserk savage;
- obvious fantasy villain.

---

## 5. Supernatural depiction

The supernatural should be unmistakable but materially integrated into the scene.

Prefer:

- smoke moving against the wind;
- a half-seen spirit-double separating from the body;
- an antlered presence echoed in mist or shadow;
- impossible changes in posture, reflection, or silhouette;
- nearby animals reacting to something unseen rather than posing as an entourage;
- fire, ash, breath, or fog responding to the Rite.

Avoid:

- glowing spell circles as the main effect;
- bright fantasy energy;
- giant spectral animals posed decoratively;
- a crowd of wolves, ravens, deer, and other stock shaman companions;
- overt magical symbols copied from real-world occult or religious systems.

The ideal supernatural effect should suggest that a transformation, crossing, or possession may be underway without confirming exactly what is happening.

---

## 6. Pose and composition

The Spirit Walker does not need the older tall, formal, staff-upright pose as his default.

Strong options include:

- crouched or kneeling at the edge of a Rite;
- rising from a low predatory posture;
- one hand extended toward smoke, flame, or an unseen presence;
- head tilted as though listening to something no one else hears;
- weight held forward, ready to move;
- asymmetrical posture that keeps the antlers and painted anatomy readable.

A ritual staff may appear, but it should be a rough branch, bone-bound implement, or inherited object rather than a generic shaman prop. It is optional, not mandatory.

For card art, preserve a clear read at small size:

1. antlers;
2. painted young human figure;
3. predatory ritual posture;
4. one restrained supernatural event.

---

## 7. Miniature direction

The miniature should preserve the revised character rather than reverting to the old portrait.

Use:

- a lean young human body;
- large readable antlers balanced for casting and tabletop durability;
- exposed torso and limbs with paint guides represented through sculpted texture only where subtle;
- a crouched, rising, or forward-poised stance;
- one dominant ritual prop at most;
- a low Rite base with ash, roots, stones, smoke, or carved stakes.

Avoid:

- an elderly upright priest pose;
- heavy robes or a layered mantle that obscures the body;
- a miniature dominated by a giant spirit creature;
- overloading the base with animal companions;
- making the antlers so large that the figure reads as a monster or becomes impractical.

---

## 8. Canonical relationship to earlier material

Where this document conflicts with earlier Spirit Walker guidance, use this document.

The following older ideas are superseded:

- the existing portrait as the final visual authority;
- a heavily clothed ceremonial-mantle silhouette;
- a primarily upright, formal invocation pose;
- a medicine-man or benevolent spirit-mediator read;
- visual language that can be mistaken for a generic Plains-indigenous character.

The following established elements remain:

- antlers;
- extensive white and dark red paint;
- ritual contact with the unseen;
- bone, wood, hide, smoke, and natural materials;
- a clear distinction from the Alchemist's experimental occult science;
- protection and endurance expressed through dangerous invocation rather than institutional authority.

---

## 9. Approval checklist

Before approving a Spirit Walker asset, confirm:

- Does he read as a young adult rather than an elder?
- Are the antlers the defining silhouette?
- Is the body paint extensive and visually important?
- Is the clothing minimal enough to preserve the painted-body design?
- Does the setting feel eastern woodland / Appalachian rather than Plains?
- Does he feel sinister, predatory, and spiritually dangerous?
- Does he remain recognizably human?
- Has the image avoided generic medicine-man, warbonnet, and fantasy-shaman shorthand?
- Is the supernatural effect unsettling but restrained?
- Does the composition remain readable at card size?
