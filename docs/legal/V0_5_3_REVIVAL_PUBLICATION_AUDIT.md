# Gauntlet v0.5.3 — 2026 Revival and Copyright Foundation Audit

**Prepared:** 2026-08-28  
**Target work:** `Gauntlet v0.5.3 Complete Playtest Edition`  
**Purpose:** Establish the first complete edition of the 2026 Gauntlet revival, distinguish creation from publication, identify the correct deposit, and define a conservative copyright-registration posture.

> Filing preparation, not a legal opinion.

## Executive conclusion

**v0.5.3 is the correct first 2026 foundation work to evaluate for registration.**

The retained June 2026 development record shows that v0.5.0, v0.5.1, and v0.5.2 were names assigned retrospectively to stages of a continuous June 17–20 redesign. They were not separately packaged public editions in the evidence presently recovered.

v0.5.3 is the first complete fixed package in that sequence:

- complete rules/playtest guide;
- 54-card canonical pool;
- 25 Territories;
- four complete 30-card / 60-point playtest decks;
- Heartlands and player aids;
- playtest forms;
- canonical structured data;
- print-and-play package.

The package was created on **June 20, 2026**. The first hard evidence presently recovered of the v0.5.3 files being made publicly available through GitHub is **June 23, 2026**. A June 22 Git commit/tag date records the local Git object date but does not by itself prove when the branch/tag was pushed to the public repository.

The author's 2026-08-28 recollection adds an earlier physical-distribution fact: during the June 21 human playtest, the author believes the other player was allowed to **keep the cards**, and that **printed rulebooks were handed out**. Because the current v0.5.4 package had been generated that morning and the recorded human playtest later that day was explicitly a v0.5.4 test, those distributed physical copies were most likely v0.5.4 rather than v0.5.3.

This materially changes the publication analysis. An unrestricted transfer/gift or lending of copies can constitute publication, but Copyright Office practice also recognizes "limited publication" where copies are distributed only to a definitely selected group for a limited purpose and without the right of further diffusion, reproduction, distribution, or sale. A social-acquaintance/playtest exchange can therefore remain unpublished depending on the restrictions and circumstances.

Accordingly, **June 21 is now the earliest plausible 2026 publication date**, but the legal classification of that playtest distribution remains unresolved.

On 2026-08-28 the author further confirmed that **no one was told the materials were private**. That weakens the case for an express restriction, but it does not by itself eliminate limited-publication treatment. Current Copyright Office Compendium guidance notes that exchanges among family members or social acquaintances can remain unpublished, and that limited publication may turn on both the selected audience and limited purpose, with express or implied restrictions being relevant rather than always mandatory.

The remaining question is therefore primarily **who received the copies and for what purpose**: a small selected playtest/friend group versus a broader distribution to people generally.

Accordingly:

- **v0.5.3 creation/fixation:** June 20;

If the June 21 distribution qualifies as publication, **v0.5.4—not v0.5.3—is likely the better first published 2026 foundation work**, because it incorporated the otherwise-unpublished v0.5.3 material plus the Last Stand revision. If the June 21 distribution was a limited publication, v0.5.3 remains the first hard public-GitHub foundation candidate on June 23.

## 4. Relationship to the 2021 work

The v0.5.3 package is **not** the origin of Gauntlet.

The public 2021 repository linked a copyable Google Sheets game document and a Tabletop Simulator Workshop mod. Historical archive reconstruction also identifies substantial older card/rule material carried into the 2026 redesign.

For a v0.5.3 filing, appreciable expression already published in the older Gauntlet work should be excluded.

However, the intermediate labels v0.5.0–v0.5.2 should **not automatically be treated as separately published preexisting works** merely because those names describe earlier stages of the June 2026 redesign. The evidence currently supports a continuous unpublished development process culminating in the complete v0.5.3 package.

Copyright Office limitation-of-claim guidance generally calls for exclusion of appreciable material that was previously published, previously registered, in the public domain, or owned by another party. Therefore, absent evidence that v0.5.0–v0.5.2 were separately published or registered, the sensible boundary is:

> **exclude pre-2026 / previously published Gauntlet expression, not every internal June 2026 draft merely because it predates the final v0.5.3 file.**

## 5. AI/human-authorship posture

The v0.5.3 prose was produced during an AI-assisted drafting workflow. The retained transcript shows that ChatGPT did substantial drafting and package assembly.

The filing should therefore **not** represent that the author personally wrote every sentence.

At the same time, the evidence strongly supports human authorship in:

- the underlying human-created game design and design revisions;
- selection of systems and alternatives after simulation;
- selection and coordination of the 54-card/25-Territory playtest corpus;
- arrangement of the complete playtest edition and its components;
- editorial scope and hierarchy;
- revisions/corrections actually supplied by the human;
- qualifying human modifications to older or AI-assisted material.

Much of the Rulebook/card language is functional rules text. Game procedures and methods are unclaimable regardless of AI involvement, reducing the practical value of reconstructing sentence-level provenance for every timing instruction.

### Conservative literary filing posture

The safest useful claim is **not** "all text."

Working claim:

- human selection, coordination, and arrangement;
- qualifying revisions and editing;
- any separately verified human-authored textual material.

Working exclusions:

- appreciable pre-2026 / previously published Gauntlet expression;
- appreciable text generated by artificial intelligence;
- pictorial material;
- third-party material;
- uncopyrightable game methods, procedures, systems, titles, and short phrases.

The v0.5.3 registration is therefore likely to be a **thin but useful foundation registration**, particularly for the 2026 compilation/editorial authorship. It should not be described as broad ownership of the mechanics or all machine-drafted language.
