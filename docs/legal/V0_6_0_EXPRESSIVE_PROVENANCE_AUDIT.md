## Executive conclusion

The remaining Rulebook authorship issue is narrow enough that it should **not block filing preparation**.

The strongest affirmative provenance evidence establishes that Tymon Scott made the substantive game-design, editorial, terminology, scope, and selection/arrangement decisions embodied in the Rulebook. The clearest appreciable block of final prose whose **exact expressive wording** was supplied by ChatGPT is the three-paragraph **Welcome to Gauntlet** introduction.

For the v0.6.0 Rulebook filing, the conservative working posture is therefore:

- identify **Tymon Scott as the sole human author**;
- claim qualifying text, revisions/editing, and selection/coordination/arrangement;
- expressly exclude the three-paragraph AI-drafted Welcome prose;
- exclude **all pictorial material** from this Literary Work claim rather than trying to establish visual provenance in the Rulebook filing;
- continue to exclude all pre-v0.6 Gauntlet material, third-party material, and uncopyrightable game methods/systems;
- do not treat short functional faction/Leader summaries or mottos as a material basis for the Rulebook claim.

This preserves the useful human-authorship claim while avoiding a false assertion that the human author personally formed every expressive sentence generated during the AI-assisted drafting process.

## 1. Controlling Copyright Office framework

The Copyright Office's current policy asks whether traditional elements of authorship were actually conceived and executed by a human or by a machine. It recognizes copyright in qualifying human selection, arrangement, and modifications even when AI-generated material is present.

For registration, AI-generated content that is more than de minimis should be identified and excluded. The presence of AI-generated material does **not** make the larger human-authored work unregistrable.

Official guidance:
- https://www.copyright.gov/ai/ai_policy_guidance.pdf
- https://www.copyright.gov/ai/

## 2. Welcome to Gauntlet — known mixed process, AI-supplied final prose

### Retained conversation evidence

The archived July 20, 2026 development conversation provides unusually clear provenance.

The process was:

1. ChatGPT proposed an introductory Rulebook passage.
2. Tymon Scott rejected the prior closing as too marketing-like and directed a more player-agency-focused approach.
3. Tymon then identified two substantive problems:
   - the opening implied the players would use different factions even though mirror matchups are legal;
   - deck construction needed greater emphasis as a meaningful part of the game.
4. ChatGPT responded with the revised three-paragraph introduction beginning:
   - `Gauntlet is a two-player tactical card game of deck construction, strategy, timing, and territorial control.`
5. The revision incorporated the human-directed mirror-match and deck-construction requirements.
6. Tymon approved the revised passage.
7. That AI-drafted prose, with later editorial normalization from second person to neutral Rulebook voice, became the July 20 publication text.

Evidence source retained in the ChatGPT export:
- `selected_transcripts_part_002.md`
- user turn timestamp: `2026-07-20T17:59:40.586089Z`
- assistant revised-opening turn: `2026-07-20T17:59:45.302419Z`

### Authorship classification

**Classification: AR for the final three-paragraph expressive prose, with substantial human creative direction and human editorial selection/arrangement around it.**

Why:

- the human supplied the substantive design facts and editorial constraints;
- the human rejected earlier wording and approved the revision;
- but the retrieved record shows ChatGPT supplied the actual sentence-level expressive wording of the final three-paragraph form;
- the later neutral-voice edits are largely functional/editorial normalization rather than a demonstrated wholesale human rewrite.

Under current Copyright Office guidance, the safest registration treatment is to **exclude the final three-paragraph Welcome prose as AI-generated text** while still claiming the human-authored overall selection, coordination, arrangement, revisions, and other qualifying text in the Rulebook.

This conclusion does **not** mean the ideas, game concepts, editorial decisions, or Rulebook structure belong to AI. AI is not an author or claimant.

## 3. Faction introductions

The six faction introductions in the July 20 Rulebook are short summaries such as:

- Military converting battle victories into Command and pressure;
- Diplomats using Influence and Terms;
- Financiers converting cards/Territory control into Capital and Deeds;
- Intelligence using Missions and Intel;
- Mystics completing Rites through sacrifice/binding/exchange;
- Inquisition converting opposing commitments/losses into Conviction.

### Evidence

The substantive faction systems were developed and approved before the consolidated Rulebook. Pre-release definitive faction guides existed between July 15 and July 18, and the July 20 Rulebook consolidated those already-developed packages.

Retained development conversations show direct human decisions governing:
- faction mechanics and victory structures;
- which material belonged in the canonical Rulebook;
- removal of speculative strategy, matchup ratings, and untested advice;
- standardized faction presentation;
- terminology corrections and package composition.

### Registration treatment

**Do not rely on the exact faction-opening one-liners as a material independent authorship claim.**

They are short, heavily functional summaries of game systems. Their copyright significance is limited compared with the Rulebook's larger human selection, editing, and arrangement.

The present archive does not establish line-by-line human authorship of those exact sentences strongly enough to label all six **H**. It likewise does not justify labeling them all **AR** merely because AI tools were involved.

Working treatment:
- leave exact sentence-level classification unnecessary for Worksheet A;
- claim the qualifying human editorial/arrangement contribution around the faction chapters;
- exclude game methods/systems;
- if later evidence affirmatively shows an appreciable faction passage was AI-generated, add it to the AI-text exclusion without changing the overall filing architecture.

## 4. Leader descriptive prose and mottos

Each v0.6.0 Leader entry contains:
- name;
- archetype;
- short motto;
- one short descriptive sentence;
- functional ability text.

The archived design work demonstrates extensive human direction over the Leaders' mechanics, archetypes, visual identities, distinctions, and inclusion in the Rulebook. However, the currently recovered evidence does not establish line-by-line human authorship of every short descriptive sentence.

### Copyright significance

The short descriptive sentences are strongly tied to the Leader's mechanical function. The mottos are very short phrases.

Accordingly, these lines should **not be treated as the core literary value of the Rulebook registration**, and there is little practical benefit in delaying the filing to reconstruct their sentence-by-sentence drafting provenance.

Working treatment:
- claim qualifying human selection, editing, arrangement, and any supported original text;
- do not rely on short mottos/titles/archetype labels as independently protectable authorship;
- treat functional Leader ability text under the same method/system limitations applied elsewhere in the Rulebook.

## 5. Pictorial material — remove from Worksheet A claim

The historical Rulebook PDF contains Leader images.

The presently recovered record is not sufficient to classify every included Leader image as:
- wholly human-created;
- AI-generated;
- or AI-derived with qualifying human modifications.

There is no reason to make that uncertainty a blocker for the **Literary Work** registration.

### Working treatment

For Worksheet A:

**Exclude all pictorial material from the claim.**

This is a scope choice, not a conclusion that the images are AI-generated.

The visual material can be evaluated separately in the card/component or visual-art registration workstream, where image provenance can be handled asset by asset without contaminating the Rulebook filing.

Candidate limitation language should therefore include:
> pictorial material

separately from:
> text generated by artificial intelligence
