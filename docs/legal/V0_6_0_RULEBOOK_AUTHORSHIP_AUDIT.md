**Purpose:** Narrow the AI/human-authorship issue to the portions of the published Rulebook where it can materially affect the copyright claim.

> This is filing preparation, not a legal opinion. It applies the project posture adopted in `AI_AUTHORSHIP_LEDGER.md`: Tymon Scott is the sole human creator/author of the overall Gauntlet work; AI involvement is not itself AI authorship.

## Executive conclusion

The v0.6.0 Rulebook should **not** be treated as a 50-page block requiring sentence-by-sentence proof that a human typed every instruction.

The published Rulebook contains three materially different kinds of content:

1. **Game methods, procedures, systems, and short functional rules.** These are wholly or largely outside copyright protection regardless of whether a human or AI chose the exact concise wording.
2. **Human selection, coordination, arrangement, revision, and editorial structure.** The development record strongly supports a human authorship claim in the Rulebook's overall architecture and editorial choices.
3. **Appreciable expressive prose.** This is where AI provenance matters most. The remaining audit should concentrate on longer introductory/descriptive prose and any other passage whose protectability depends materially on the expression itself.

The practical result is a much smaller remaining authorship problem. There is presently **no basis to disclaim an entire chapter merely because generative AI participated in its development**.

## 1. Strong human-authorship evidence already established

Retained conversation/development history establishes affirmative human control over numerous editorial and structural choices embodied in the July 20 Rulebook. Examples include:

- requiring the Rulebook to emphasize pre-game deck construction and permit mirror faction/Leader matchups;
- distinguishing the overall **Deck** from the **Playable Deck** and in-game **Draw Pile**;
- requiring Leader-specific supplemental components to be part of the Leader/game package without entering the Playable Deck;
- rejecting a mulligan;
- specifying one die per player as the ordinary component expectation and a roll-off for first player;
- specifying start-of-turn capture when occupying a Territory not controlled, without an additional "since last turn" condition;
- correcting the battle-card lifecycle so hand commitments normally enter the Graveyard while Battle Hand cards normally enter the Discard Pile;
- requiring the canonical Rulebook to remain objective rather than include matchup advice, difficulty ratings, card rankings, or speculative strategy claims;
- directing terminology normalization and the distinction among movement, withdrawal, retreat, occupation, control, and capture;
- directing omission of a credits page from the v0.6.0 Rulebook.

These facts do not make every sentence human-authored by themselves. They do, however, strongly support the human authorship of **selection, coordination, arrangement, editorial scope, terminology architecture, and many substantive revisions**.

## 2. Copyright significance is not uniform across the Rulebook

The audit should not spend equal effort on every line.

### Low copyright significance: functional rules

Examples include:

- deck-size and deck-value limits;
- setup sequence;
- turn order;
- battle procedure;
- movement and capture procedure;
- zone destinations;
- numeric resource limits;
- faction timing procedures;
- glossary definitions that merely state game functions;
- Quick Reference procedure lists.

The underlying methods, procedures, systems, facts, titles, short phrases, and similar matter are not protected merely because they appear in the Rulebook. Some sufficiently original explanatory wording around them may still be protectable, but the scope is inherently narrower.

For filing purposes, these passages generally do **not** justify a forensic sentence-by-sentence AI audit unless they contain appreciable explanatory or expressive prose beyond the functional rule.

### High copyright significance: expressive prose

The passages most worth provenance review are:

- the three-paragraph **Welcome to Gauntlet** introduction;
- faction-opening descriptive paragraphs where they go beyond stating mechanics;
- Leader descriptive prose;
- longer explanatory passages that frame a mechanic in expressive rather than merely procedural terms;
- any substantial nonfunctional prose embedded in the Rulebook;
- qualifying visual/graphic material included in the deposited PDF.

Short **Motto** lines and isolated titles should not be treated as major copyright assets merely because they are stylistically expressive; short phrases are ordinarily outside the claim.

## 4. Why the faction chapters are mixed rather than presumptively AI-generated

The six faction chapters were assembled from previously developed faction packages into a single objective Rulebook.

The human editorial contribution includes:

- deciding that all six factions belong in the canonical Rulebook;
- choosing the chapter order;
- deciding what faction information belonged in a Rulebook versus strategy material;
- excluding matchup predictions, rankings, difficulty assessments, and strategy-guide content;
- integrating shared terminology across otherwise separate faction systems;
- deciding which Leader mechanics and supplemental components were required for complete play;
- approving/correcting the faction frameworks throughout development.

That supports a meaningful claim in selection, coordination, arrangement, and qualifying editing even where a particular descriptive sentence may ultimately require an AI disclaimer.

The remaining task is therefore **not** "prove the faction chapters were written without AI." It is "identify any appreciable descriptive prose whose final expressive wording was substantially supplied by AI and remained without sufficient human expressive contribution."

## 5. Visual material in the Rulebook PDF

The literary-work filing should remain distinct from the question of whether every embedded visual is copyrightable.

The publication PDF contains Leader imagery and production design. For any deposited pictorial material:

- claim human-created visual authorship only where supported;
- claim sufficiently original human modifications to AI source imagery only where supported;
- exclude appreciable substantially AI-generated imagery;
- exclude third-party visual material.

An AI-generated image appearing inside an otherwise registrable literary work does not require treating the Rulebook itself as AI-authored. It requires an accurate limitation of claim.

## 6. Working registration posture after this pass

Unless the remaining expressive-prose review reveals a substantially different provenance pattern, the Rulebook application can continue toward:

**Human author**
- Tymon Scott

**Author Created**
- Text
- Revisions and editing
- Selection, coordination, and arrangement

**Material Excluded**
- Preexisting Gauntlet material
- Appreciable text and pictorial material generated by artificial intelligence
- Third-party material
- Uncopyrightable game methods and systems

**New Material Included**
- New and revised text
- Revisions and modifications
- Selection, coordination, and arrangement

The final application should not use "AI-generated text" as a blanket description of rules language merely because AI was used in the drafting workflow.

## 7. Targeted provenance review — resolved for Worksheet A

The follow-up audit in `docs/legal/V0_6_0_EXPRESSIVE_PROVENANCE_AUDIT.md` resolves this filing pass as follows:

- **Welcome to Gauntlet:** the final three-paragraph sentence-level prose was supplied by ChatGPT after substantive human direction/correction; exclude that introductory prose as AI-generated text.
- **Faction introductions and Leader descriptive prose:** short and predominantly functional; exact line-by-line provenance is not relied on as a principal literary claim and does not justify delaying Worksheet A.
- **Mottos/titles/archetype labels:** short matter and not relied on as material independent authorship.
- **Rulebook pictorial material:** exclude all pictorial material from Worksheet A as a scope choice; evaluate visuals in the separate card/visual workstream.
- **Other functional rules:** no forensic sentence-by-sentence provenance review required for this filing posture.

The Rulebook's claimed human authorship remains qualifying text, revisions/editing, and selection/coordination/arrangement, subject to the stated exclusions.

## 8. Filing consequence

This pass materially reduces the authorship blocker identified in the earlier filing worksheet.

We do **not** need to reconstruct the provenance of every setup instruction, timing sentence, numeric limit, glossary definition, or Quick Reference bullet before filing. Those portions are predominantly functional and/or already supported by strong human editorial evidence.

The remaining high-value inquiry is bounded: **appreciable expressive prose and visual material**.

That is the appropriate place to spend provenance effort before certifying the application.
